# Game Concepts — 2026-11-09

**Target dev:** Solo indie, 1–2 years Unity, working on a wave survival shooter.

**Research signals that shaped this batch:**
- "Remember November 2026" / AI polar bear meme is peaking on TikTok & Reels, cursed-liminal vibe.
- Stranger Things S5 Vol. 3 finale lands this month → D&D / 80s nostalgia / Vecna-raid wave.
- GTA VI launches Nov 19 — every mid-sized release is getting crushed under its shadow; "small, snackable, viral" is where oxygen is.
- Steam officially added the "Bullet Heaven" tag in May 2026, Bullet Heaven Fest exists now, but the cosmetic-clone flood of 2023–24 has receded — room for *mechanical* twists on the genre again.
- Roblox's "Steal a Brainrot" + Italian Brainrot meme → absurd, loud, 60-second-session design is the hot aesthetic.
- Recurring r/Games wish: more diegetic UI (Firewatch / Dead Space style) — still under-explored in shooters.
- "2026 is the new 2016" nostalgia cycle; FIFA World Cup 2026 is live this summer.
- Underserved: couch co-op is near dead on Steam; party wave-shooters are a gap.

## TREND-BASED (riding current cultural wave)

### 1. Remember November

1. **Pitch:** Defend an abandoned Arctic research station through a seven-night loop while AI-generated polar bears sing hymns at you from the ice.
2. **Core mechanic:** Wave shooter where enemies arrive to *music* — the AI polar-bear chorus is both ambience and attack telegraph. Shoot off-beat and you get interrupted; shoot on-beat and you get a combo multiplier. Between waves, scavenge the station in first person for fuel/ammo/tapes.
3. **Why now?** The "Remember November 2026" meme is in its peak-saturation window on TikTok/Reels right now. Cursed-liminal arctic imagery + AI-video texture is a readymade visual identity, and TikTok creators will make the trailer for you if the vibe is right.
4. **Solo feasibility:** 8/10. First-person wave shooter in a single enclosed snowy map is a Unity sweet spot. Hardest part is the AI-video aesthetic without actually using AI slop — stylize it as VHS-tape glitch instead.
5. **Scope:** 6 months to vertical slice + 7-night loop. 9–10 months to a polished release with 3 maps.
6. **Closest existing game:** *Voices of the Void* for vibe; *Devour* for the night-loop ritual; *Project Zomboid* for scavenging cadence.
7. **Potential fatal flaw:** The meme burns out before launch. Mitigation: design so the game *works* as a cursed-arctic horror shooter even if nobody remembers the meme in 6 months — the meme is the marketing hook, not the gameplay.

### 2. Brainrot Battalion

1. **Pitch:** A 60-second-per-run bullet heaven where you play as absurd Italian Brainrot creatures defending a pasta factory from waves of knockoff brainrot.
2. **Core mechanic:** Classic vampire-survivors auto-attack loop, but every character is a meme creature with a loud, exaggerated signature weapon (Tralalero's sneakers, Bombardiro's bombs, etc.). Each 60-second run earns one meta-upgrade — the whole game is designed around TikTok clip length.
3. **Why now?** Roblox's "Steal a Brainrot" cracked 64B visits on a near-zero-production-value loop. There's clearly audience appetite but no polished premium Steam entry — Roblox players age out and want a "real" version.
4. **Solo feasibility:** 9/10. Bullet heaven is the most-documented solo genre on Earth; the art is intentionally cursed, so a solo dev's weird style is a feature.
5. **Scope:** 3 months to Early Access with 6 characters. 6 months for 1.0 with 15+.
6. **Closest existing game:** *Vampire Survivors* mechanically; *Steal a Brainrot* (Roblox) for audience; *Goat Simulator* for tonal reference.
7. **Potential fatal flaw:** IP/legal risk is real — the "Italian Brainrot" characters' creators have been filing copyright suits since Nov 2025 (Bloomberg covered it in March 2026). Must use *your own* brainrot creatures, not the famous ones. Also: brainrot aesthetic ages in months, not years.

### 3. Hawkins '84

1. **Pitch:** Co-op wave shooter where four kids on bikes defend a small Indiana town from Upside-Down incursions over seven Saturday-night sessions.
2. **Core mechanic:** Wave survival + light D&D-style class system (Paladin/Rogue/Bard/Wizard mapped to flashlight-tank/stealth-scout/radio-support/slingshot-DPS). Between waves you *roll dice* at the campaign table in a kid's basement to decide which part of town gets attacked next.
3. **Why now?** Stranger Things S5 Vol. 3 finale drops this month; D&D saw a 140% player bump after S4. Netflix marketing is doing the cultural work for you through December.
4. **Solo feasibility:** 7/10. Four-character co-op is work, but the 80s-small-town aesthetic is forgiving and Unity's URP handles it well. Biggest risk is netcode — ship *local* co-op first.
5. **Scope:** 6 months to a solo + local co-op vertical slice. 10–12 months for online co-op.
6. **Closest existing game:** *Stranger Things 3: The Game* (official, 2019, well-received); *Project Zomboid* for town-under-siege; *The Front Room* for 80s feel.
7. **Potential fatal flaw:** Living in another show's shadow with no license. If you lean too hard into Stranger Things visuals you get a takedown; if you lean away, nobody makes the connection. Must find your own identifiable twist (e.g. "it's set during the actual D&D session the kids are playing, and the map reshapes around their rolls").

## GAP-IN-MARKET (nobody's done this well yet)

### 4. Hands-On

1. **Pitch:** A wave survival shooter with zero HUD — ammo is on the magazine, health is on your character's body, objectives are notes you pick up and read.
2. **Core mechanic:** Everything the player needs to know is in the world. Reload by physically slapping a new mag in (gesture mapped to R). Health is visible as blood and limp animation. The map is a paper map you pull out with Tab, which pauses nothing. Waves still come at you, hordes still spawn, but you *feel* every piece of info.
3. **Why now?** r/Games keeps upvoting "I wish more games had diegetic UI" posts (top post Feb 2026). Dead Space Remake and Firewatch proved the appetite; no one has tried this in the wave-shooter genre. Also: GTA VI's maximalist HUD will make minimalist competitors feel refreshing.
4. **Solo feasibility:** 8/10. Technically simpler than normal UI once you commit — fewer menus to design. Animation-heavy though; one strong rigged character does a lot of work.
5. **Scope:** 6 months. The constraint actually shortens scope rather than expanding it.
6. **Closest existing game:** *Dead Space* (franchise); *Hunt: Showdown* for diegetic reloads; *Receiver 2* for brutal-realism gun handling.
7. **Potential fatal flaw:** Streamers can't tell what's happening in the gameplay clip. Must design at least *one* unmistakable visual signal per event so a TikTok viewer gets it in 2 seconds.

### 5. Couch Horde

1. **Pitch:** A 4-player split-screen wave shooter built specifically for one couch, one TV, four controllers.
2. **Core mechanic:** Vampire-Survivors-style auto-attack so players don't need to aim precisely (makes split-screen playable), but *positioning* is everything — you collide with teammates, revive them by standing near, and share pickups. Think Overcooked's spatial chaos but with bullets.
3. **Why now?** Couch co-op is a dying category on Steam — *Helldivers 2* has none, *Vampire Survivors* local-co-op is weak. There's a "dad plays with kid on weekends" audience actively complaining about this gap. Steam Deck + living-room TV is also having a moment.
4. **Solo feasibility:** 7/10. Split-screen rendering in URP is not free but is well-documented. Input handling for 4 pads is Unity's Input System's bread and butter.
5. **Scope:** 6 months to a solid 1.0 with 10 characters and 5 arenas. Online co-op deliberately deferred to a post-launch "if it sells" problem.
6. **Closest existing game:** *Castle Crashers*, *Gauntlet* (2014), *Vampire Survivors* local-co-op.
7. **Potential fatal flaw:** The addressable market is small and mostly buys on console, where Unity solo-dev console cert is painful. Mitigate by targeting Steam Deck as a "mini-console" first and designing for TV-friendly readability from day one.

### 6. Chess-Shooter

1. **Pitch:** Wave survival where you aim *once* at the start of each wave, lock your fire arc, then spend the wave moving and upgrading while your character auto-shoots along the arc.
2. **Core mechanic:** Positioning/geometry puzzle on top of survivor-like upgrades. Each wave you draw a cone of fire; enemies inside it die, outside it live. Between waves, buy upgrades that reshape the cone (split into two, wrap around you, orbit) — Auto Chess economy meets Vampire Survivors.
3. **Why now?** Bullet Heaven is an officially recognized Steam genre now (May 2026), the clone flood receded, and the audience is ready for *mechanical* evolution rather than more skin-swap clones. Auto-battler audience is desperate for new entries post-TFT fatigue.
4. **Solo feasibility:** 9/10. Core mechanic is literally "a cone that kills things in it" — trivial to prototype. All the depth is in the upgrade economy, which is design work, not engine work.
5. **Scope:** 3 months to a surprisingly complete demo. 6 months to a 1.0 with depth.
6. **Closest existing game:** *Vampire Survivors* (closest), *Mechabellum* (positioning auto-battler), *Brotato* (shop loop).
7. **Potential fatal flaw:** "Locked aim" sounds boring in a store listing. The genre is player-skill-starved and a pitch that *removes* twitch aiming might filter out the audience before they try it. Trailer must show the *second-to-second* decision density, not the arc-locking.

## WILDCARD / EXPERIMENTAL

### 7. Decrescendo

1. **Pitch:** A wave shooter where you shrink one notch every wave survived — enemies stay the same size — and the arena slowly becomes a cathedral around a mouse.
2. **Core mechanic:** Standard wave combat early on. Around wave 5 you're pigeon-sized and must shelter in cover; by wave 10 you're mouse-sized and the former "bullets" are now artillery shells; by wave 15 you're a speck and must fight asymmetrically with traps and environment. Weapons, controls, and even sound mix rescale with you.
3. **Why now?** "Size comparison" and "scale shift" content is doing massive numbers on TikTok and Instagram (universe size, animal size, etc.). No one's turned it into a game loop. It's also a *built-in* trailer hook — show wave 1 vs. wave 15 side by side.
4. **Solo feasibility:** 6/10. Scaling physics/audio cleanly in Unity is more work than it sounds, and 3–4 weapon redesigns per scale tier is real content. But a short game is viable.
5. **Scope:** 6 months if scoped tight (one arena, 15 scale tiers, one character). 1 year with multiple arenas.
6. **Closest existing game:** *Grounded* for scale premise; *Hades* for runs-with-twists; nothing really does the "shrink-per-wave" loop.
7. **Potential fatal flaw:** The novelty might be a 30-minute trailer moment that wears off in-game. Must design the scale tiers to each feel like a *different genre of shooter*, not just "same gun smaller."

### 8. One Button

1. **Pitch:** A wave shooter controlled entirely by one button — hold to charge, tap to fire, timing to dodge — designed to be played one-handed on the subway.
2. **Core mechanic:** Character auto-moves on a track or pattern; the single input overloads based on context (hold = charge shot, release = fire, double-tap = roll, hold + release during enemy wind-up = parry). Depth comes from reading enemy telegraphs under a very constrained input budget.
3. **Why now?** Mobile shooter market is a wasteland of ad-infested slop. Accessibility is a growing conversation; one-button is the most honest expression of it. Short-form attention spans mean "1 button, 2-min runs" fits.
4. **Solo feasibility:** 9/10. Minimal controls = minimal animation state space. Cross-platform (mobile + Switch + Steam Deck) with no control remapping headaches.
5. **Scope:** 3 months to a polished free-with-ads mobile launch; 6 months to a premium Steam/Switch version.
6. **Closest existing game:** *One Finger Death Punch* (one-button combat, sold ~1M+), *Downwell* (minimal inputs), *Holedown* (single-input depth).
7. **Potential fatal flaw:** "One button" filters out the hardcore shooter audience that follows wave survival content. Needs a dedicated hook that isn't about minimalism — e.g., "the whole game fits on the lock screen widget."
