# Game Concepts — 2026-11-23

Solo Unity dev, 1–2 years experience, currently on a wave-survival shooter. Concepts biased toward reusing those strengths (spawn systems, feel, run-based loops) and away from heavy netcode / open-world scope.

## Signal scan (what I was reading when I generated these)

- **Meccha Chameleon** — hide-and-seek where you paint yourself to blend; blew up on YouTube/Twitch mid-2026.
- **Peak** — 4-player co-op climbing, still trending late 2026.
- **Buckshot Roulette** — single-screen, two-chair horror; format still mines a vein.
- **Tung** — niche first-person hedge-maze horror; a reminder the maze subgenre is under-mined.
- **CloverPit / Megabonk** — 2025 viral indies in the hundred-thousand to million-sales range; both are tight, legible, streamable loops.
- **Vampire Survivors / bullet heaven** — saturated at the median, still rewards a *mechanical* twist (Nimrods-style crafting, Megabonk's 3D framing).
- **TikTok, Nov 2026** — "makeup loading % transition" (peaking), "piggyback flip" (rising), "my moon my man" couple montage (fresh). Día de los Muertos / Movember / UK Bonfire Night anchor the calendar.
- **Netflix, Nov 2026** — holiday-horror vibe (RE1/RE2 films added, Insidious 2–3, *A Dog's Perfect Christmas*); *Black Doves* S2 and *The Hunting Wives* S2 for thriller mood.
- **Market gaps flagged by analysts** — racing co-op (high rating, low supply), "games for core players the big studios aren't making anymore," and subgenre-specific cozy variants beyond farming.

## Trend-based (3)

### 1. Movember Survivors

- **Pitch:** A roguelite where your mustache *is* your weapon, your HP bar, and your progression tree — grow it all November, lose it all December.
- **Core mechanic:** Wave-survival auto-shooter. Enemies drop "grooming" pickups. Style points level up your 'stache into archetypes (Handlebar = AoE sweep, Horseshoe = chain whip, Pencil = piercing laser, Walrus = shield). Running out of style = clean-shaven = run over.
- **Why now:** Movember is a real annual cultural event with zero good game attached. Releases in Nov 2027 ride a yearly tide forever; charity tie-in gives press a hook.
- **Solo feasibility:** 9/10. Mechanically a reskin/evolution of what the dev already builds. Art is stylized and reusable.
- **Scope:** 3–4 months to Early Access.
- **Closest existing:** Vampire Survivors, Nimrods: GunCraft Survivor.
- **Fatal flaw:** The joke is the whole game. If the stache-as-stat metaphor doesn't carry mechanically past hour two, the premise is a skin, not a game.

### 2. Blend In

- **Pitch:** Single-player stealth-painting game: paint yourself into a diorama before the automated hunter-camera finishes its sweep.
- **Core mechanic:** Each level is a tiny shoebox diorama (kitchen, library, aquarium). You have a palette and a 15-second brush window; a hunter-camera then scans the room and flags anything that doesn't match. 60 handcrafted rooms, daily seed.
- **Why now:** Meccha Chameleon proved streamers love the paint-to-blend read. Going *single-player* sidesteps the netcode-heavy area Meccha owns.
- **Solo feasibility:** 7/10. Content-heavy (rooms), but each room is tiny and reusable. No multiplayer.
- **Scope:** 5–6 months.
- **Closest existing:** Meccha Chameleon, Hidden Folks (inverted).
- **Fatal flaw:** The core joy of Meccha is social — streamer viewers spotting the player. A single-player version has to replace that loop with something equally gif-able, or it reads as the solo-dev-couldn't-afford-multiplayer version of Meccha.

### 3. Doom Scroll

- **Pitch:** A portrait-aspect wave shooter that looks and feels like a TikTok feed — every run is one "post," and scrolling up loads the next.
- **Core mechanic:** Thumb-on-left-edge aim, right-edge auto-fire. Each 90-second run has its own licensable-feeling royalty-free track, caption, and "creator." Dying scrolls to the next run; you can "follow" procedural creators to bias what you see.
- **Why now:** Vertical-aspect PC games (Suika Game, Downwell on phones) are quietly a real category. Phone-shape on Steam is still a novelty hook that streamers screenshot.
- **Solo feasibility:** 8/10. UI is the hard part, not the combat.
- **Scope:** 4 months.
- **Closest existing:** Downwell, Holedown.
- **Fatal flaw:** Positioning. Looks free-to-play, priced premium. The feed metaphor has to be legibly a *joke about* feeds, not an actual feed, or players bounce expecting mobile F2P.

## Gap in market (3)

### 4. Belay

- **Pitch:** Peak, but descending — two-player asymmetric co-op where one climbs down a collapsing tower and the other controls the rope from the top.
- **Core mechanic:** Climber is first-person, limited stamina, grabs holds. Belayer is top-down, pays out rope, swings the climber past hazards, and chooses when to cut dead weight. Each floor collapses on a timer from the top.
- **Why now:** Peak proved the appetite for co-op vertical traversal. Going *downward* + asymmetric is a real design wedge, not a reskin.
- **Solo feasibility:** 5/10. 2-player co-op is the wall; rope physics is the second wall.
- **Scope:** 10–12 months if netcode is in scope; 6 if local-only + Steam Remote Play.
- **Closest existing:** Peak, A Difficult Game About Climbing, Lovers in a Dangerous Spacetime (for the asymmetry).
- **Fatal flaw:** Rope physics in Unity is a research project. Ship with a cable that feels like a cable, or it feels like dragging a stick.

### 5. Pit Stop

- **Pitch:** Local/online co-op racing where one player drives blind and the other is the entire pit crew, in real time, while the car is on the track.
- **Core mechanic:** Driver sees road only. Co-pilot has tires/engine/fuel/damage HUD, swipes in parts mid-lap through a Overcooked-style minigame rack. Miscommunication is the content.
- **Why now:** MIDiA and others flag racing co-op as a high-rating, low-supply niche. No one's actually built it well. Also plays into the F1/Drive-to-Survive durable TV audience.
- **Solo feasibility:** 6/10. Arcade car feel is a significant skill the dev doesn't have yet, but one well-tuned car on 4 tracks is enough.
- **Scope:** 8–10 months.
- **Closest existing:** Overcooked crossed with Art of Rally. No direct competitor — that's the point and the risk.
- **Fatal flaw:** Car handling. Indies underestimate it every time. Without months on feel alone, the whole game feels like a prototype.

### 6. The Hedge

- **Pitch:** A hedge-maze horror where the walls only move when you blink.
- **Core mechanic:** First-person; a long-press "blink" button closes your eyes for ~0.4s and the maze rearranges. You *have* to blink — a stamina-like "dry eye" meter forces it periodically. Something in the maze also only moves when you can't see.
- **Why now:** Tung and its cousins showed there's streamer demand for short, tight first-person maze horror. Mazes are strangely under-represented right now.
- **Solo feasibility:** 9/10. Tiny asset count (hedges, sky, one creature), one clear mechanic, procedural level gen is well-trodden.
- **Scope:** 3 months to a 2-hour game.
- **Closest existing:** Tung, SCP-087, Iron Lung (for the "one mechanic, tight" shape).
- **Fatal flaw:** One-trick mechanic. If "blink to shuffle" doesn't branch into 3–4 real variations (forced blinks, blink-triggered enemies, the-maze-blinks-back), the game is a 30-minute demo stretched to 2 hours.

## Wildcard / experimental (2)

### 7. Lights Out, Cul-de-Sac

- **Pitch:** Fixed-camera survival horror set in one American suburban cul-de-sac on Christmas Eve, where a holiday light infestation is rewiring the neighbors.
- **Core mechanic:** Classic RE-likes: tank-ish movement, inventory tetris, limited saves. The gimmick is that light levels in each yard change what's hostile — unplug a house and its creature dies, but the neighbor's wakes up.
- **Why now:** Netflix just added RE1/RE2 films and Insidious 2/3 in Nov 2026; *A Dog's Perfect Christmas* is dropping the same month. Holiday horror is having a measured moment. Signalis and Crow Country proved there's an audience for small-scope RE-likes.
- **Solo feasibility:** 7/10. Fixed-camera pre-rendered backgrounds are a solo dev's friend — no camera control code, 2D art hides detail.
- **Scope:** 10–12 months.
- **Closest existing:** Signalis, Crow Country, Alisa.
- **Fatal flaw:** Tone whiplash. The Christmas+horror pitch has to commit to menacing-cozy or mean-funny; splitting the difference reads as asset-flip.

### 8. Guy

- **Pitch:** You are Guy Fawkes. It is November 5. Everything is about to go very badly. Build a bonfire in a British village before sunset by stealing effigies of yourself from the villagers who want to burn you on it.
- **Core mechanic:** Day/night wave structure. By day, an immersive-sim-lite village where you scavenge wood and nick effigies. At night, waves of torch-wielding villagers swarm your pyre — this is where the dev's wave-shooter chops do the heavy lifting. Each night your pyre is bigger and louder, which attracts more villagers.
- **Why now:** Bonfire Night is a durable, Instagram-visible UK/Commonwealth event every November 5. Nobody's made the obvious game. "Is it treason yet?" is a hook.
- **Solo feasibility:** 9/10. Reskins the dev's existing engine work as the nighttime layer; daytime is a simple sandbox.
- **Scope:** 4–6 months.
- **Closest existing:** Don't Starve (day/night), They Are Billions (night defense), Graveyard Keeper (morbid cozy loop).
- **Fatal flaw:** Regional ceiling. UK/Commonwealth players get the joke instantly; US audience may just see "farming sim with pitchforks" and move on. Needs a tagline that works even if you've never heard of Guy Fawkes.

## Ranked for *this* dev

If I'm picking two to prototype this week, given the stated profile (solo, 1–2 yrs Unity, currently on a wave shooter):

1. **Movember Survivors** — nearest to existing competence, shortest path to a shippable demo, annual re-release cadence.
2. **The Hedge** — forces a genre switch (first-person horror) which is a valuable second string, but stays tiny.

Belay and Pit Stop are the highest ceiling but the highest risk — don't start them as your next project; start them as your project *after* shipping one of the 3-month ones.
