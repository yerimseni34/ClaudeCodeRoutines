# Game Concepts — 2026-10-26

Target developer: solo indie, 1–2 yrs Unity, building a wave survival shooter.
Mix: 3 trend-based · 3 gap-in-market · 2 wildcard/experimental.

Research signals used (late Oct 2026):
- Steam officially minted the **Bullet Heaven** tag in May 2026 after Vampire Survivors–likes went supernova; Spell Brigade shipped 1.0, PUNK/Carouspell/O3 queued up — the pond is now crowded but legit.
- **"Preparing for my Netflix documentary"** meme is one of the dominant TikTok formats of 2026 — confessional-style parody of true-crime docs.
- **Milan–Cortina Winter Olympics (Feb 2026)** was meme-gold earlier this year and will cycle again on-season in Q1 2027.
- **House of the Dragon S3** + **Avengers: Doomsday (Dec 18)** are the holiday-season tentpoles — fantasy-political and super-hero fatigue respectively.
- Survival-craft, colony sim, horror, and cozy are the sub-genres repeatedly flagged as high-revenue / big-pub-abandoned.
- Short-session horror and narrative-fitness are repeatedly listed as "nobody made this well yet."
- Vampire Survivors–like structure now expected by shooter audiences — pure wave defense without roguelite meta reads dated.

## Category A — Trend-Based (3)

### 1. True Crime: My Last Stand

**Pitch:** A roguelite wave shooter where every death is instantly re-cut into a mock Netflix true-crime documentary, with AI-generated narration psychoanalyzing your build choices.

**Core mechanic:** Standard twin-stick wave survival loop (Vampire Survivors–adjacent, projectile-based). The twist: during the run the game silently logs your decisions — weapons passed up, close calls, who finally killed you. On game-over it stitches a 20-second "documentary short" with slow-mo replay, dramatic score, and voice-over ("Friends say Mara always chose the shotgun. Experts disagree."). Clips are shareable.

**Why now?** The "Preparing for my Netflix documentary" meme is the dominant 2026 confessional-style format; share-friendly death screens are a known TikTok acquisition engine (see: Only Up, Content Warning).

**Solo dev feasibility:** 7/10. Core loop is well-trodden Unity territory. The documentary generator is where risk lives — pre-written line templates with Mad Libs–style slots keep it tractable; a full LLM/TTS integration does not.

**Scope:** 6 months with templated narration. 1 year if you want real TTS + varied camera cuts.

**Closest existing:** Vampire Survivors + Content Warning's share-first death loop.

**Fatal flaw:** The meme has a half-life. If "Netflix documentary" cools before launch, the whole conceit reads cringe. Hedge by making the mock-doc generator genre-agnostic (true crime today, war doc tomorrow).

### 2. Biathlon of the Dead

**Pitch:** A Winter Olympics–themed wave shooter: ski between arenas on a cross-country track, stop to snipe zombie waves, your heart-rate decides your aim.

**Core mechanic:** The map is a loop of ~6 shooting stations connected by ski sections. Skiing is a rhythm/stamina mini-game that doubles as reload; shooting is a precision wave fight where your crosshair wobble is tied to the stamina you spent getting there. Push too hard between stations and you miss shots; rest too long and the horde catches up.

**Why now?** Milan–Cortina was meme-gold in Feb 2026 and Winter sports stay relevant through the 2027 season — launching Q1 2027 catches the next on-season cycle. Biathlon as a mechanic (shoot-while-exhausted) has basically never been in a game.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 6/10. Two distinct gameplay modes (skiing + shooting) in one Unity project doubles asset and tuning work. Spline-based skiing on terrain is a known pain point for Unity solo devs.

**Scope:** 9–12 months. The dual-mode design makes 3 months unrealistic.

**Closest existing:** Nothing direct. Alba's skiing + Hotline Miami's wave arenas is the closest spiritual mix.

**Fatal flaw:** Olympics branding is lawyer-expensive. Must stay generic ("Alpine Games," no rings, no real nations). Also: skiing controls that feel bad will kill the whole game regardless of how good the shooting is.

### 3. Bullet Heaven: Overtime

**Pitch:** A bullet-heaven where you're a laid-off office worker defending the last cubicle in a collapsing WeWork from waves of AI-generated middle managers.

**Core mechanic:** Pure Vampire Survivors structure — auto-attacking, pick-two-of-three level-ups, 20-minute runs. Weapons are office supplies (stapler = shotgun, printer = flamethrower, Slack notifications = homing missile). Meta-progression is "unionizing" other NPCs between runs.

**Why now?** Bullet Heaven became an official Steam tag in May 2026 — Valve-sanctioned discoverability. 2026 corporate layoff coverage + AI-at-work anxiety is a well-documented live wire. "Satirical corporate" theming is proven (Lethal Company, The Stanley Parable) and genre-fresh in bullet heaven, where every third entry is medieval/sci-fi.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 9/10. This is the single most Unity-friendly genre that exists. Entity-component-system patterns are documented into the ground. Art can be flat-shaded low-poly or 2D sprite.

**Scope:** 3–4 months to a Steam EA. 6 months to 1.0.

**Closest existing:** Spell Brigade, Brotato — direct competitors. Theme is the differentiator.

**Fatal flaw:** The genre is now oversaturated. Steam charts show ~1 new bullet-heaven release per week. Without a distinct hook beyond theme (and theme fades), it drowns in the "new & trending" tab the week it ships.

## Category B — Gap in Market (3)

### 4. Rooted

**Pitch:** A cozy wave-defense game where you don't kill — you plant, lure, feed, and politely redirect wandering creatures away from your grandmother's garden.

**Core mechanic:** Enemies (rabbits, deer, slugs, raccoons) spawn in waves and path toward crops. You place lures (compost piles, flower patches, water bowls) that pull them into non-destructive side-areas. Your "weapons" are gentle: a watering can that stuns, a wind chime that confuses, a nap-inducing lavender bed. Successful waves "befriend" creatures that then help you against future waves.

**Why now?** Cozy was the fastest-growing Steam category in 2024–25 (releases ~2x year-over-year). Wave defense is dominated by violence; a pacifist entry is a genuine white space. "Cozy horror" (Cult of the Lamb, Grave Seasons) proved cozy aesthetics ride on hard mechanics.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 8/10. Low combat VFX budget, 2D top-down art is tractable, no gore or ragdoll physics. Pathfinding + lure logic is where real engineering lives, but Unity's NavMesh handles 80% of it.

**Scope:** 4–6 months. The art budget is the real timeline risk; use a defined palette and ~20 creature sprites.

**Closest existing:** Plants vs. Zombies (same shape, violent) + Stardew Valley (same tone, no waves). Nothing sits in the middle.

**Fatal flaw:** Cozy audiences skew toward long-session relaxation; wave defense is inherently stressful. If the "waves" don't feel stakes-lite enough, you alienate both audiences at once.

### 5. Horde: The Other Side

**Pitch:** A wave shooter where you ARE the horde — command a growing swarm of monsters trying to overrun a human encampment, one 15-minute run at a time.

**Core mechanic:** You're a single lead unit at first; killed humans join your swarm. Each wave you choose a mutation (faster swarm, armored swarm, flying scouts) and the humans dig in with new defenses. Reversed Vampire Survivors — you *are* the auto-attacking blob now. End-of-run meta-progression lets you unlock new monster leaders.

**Why now?** The "play as the monster" fantasy keeps surfacing on Reddit ("I wish there was a game where I'm the zombies"). Carrion was a hit in a different genre. Nobody has done it in the bullet-heaven shape, even though structurally it's identical with inverted camera.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 8/10. Same ECS tech as a regular bullet heaven. Human AI defenders are the new work vs. a vanilla survivors-like, but they're stationary towers for most waves.

**Scope:** 4–5 months.

**Closest existing:** They Are Billions (RTS, different genre); nothing in the bullet-heaven subgenre has done the inversion.

**Fatal flaw:** Power fantasy and swarm aesthetics overlap awkwardly — if the player can't *see* their individual contribution in the blob, the fantasy evaporates. Camera and unit silhouette design will make or break it.

### 6. Last Call

**Pitch:** A narrative wave survival shooter where each run is a chapter in a bartender's story, and the enemies you fight are literal manifestations of your regulars' problems.

**Core mechanic:** Day runs = serve drinks, hear stories, pick dialogue (visual-novel-lite). Night runs = fight waves whose enemy types and arena layout are *derived from the day's conversations*. Comforted the war vet? Night features flashback-trench waves. Ignored the anxious teen? She shows up as a boss. 7-day week structure, 30-ish minutes per in-game day.

**Why now?** "Short-session narrative" is a repeatedly cited white space; so is "a game that treats bar-tending as more than Coffee Talk." Combining a wave shooter with a narrative hub is a structural gap — Hades did it with isometric brawling and nobody has done it with wave shooters.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 5/10. Narrative writing is the hidden cost. A solo dev with 1–2 years Unity typically underestimates branching dialogue by 3–5×.

**Scope:** 9–12 months, with scope cuts. Ink or Yarn Spinner is mandatory, not optional.

**Closest existing:** Coffee Talk (narrative only) + Hades (narrative + action loop). Nothing combines bar-sim with wave combat.

**Fatal flaw:** You'll ship either a mediocre narrative stapled to a decent shooter, or vice versa. The whole premise requires both halves to be good, and a solo dev usually can only pull off one.

## Category C — Wildcard / Experimental (2)

### 7. SCREAM

**Pitch:** A wave shooter where every ability is bound to a different kind of vocal sound — scream to dash, hum to heal, whisper to go invisible.

**Core mechanic:** Microphone-driven input layer on top of a conventional WASD + mouse shooter. Loudness, pitch, and duration map to ~6 distinct abilities. Cooldowns are enforced by vocal fatigue (the game tracks when you've screamed recently and locks it out). Streamer-native: Twitch chat sees a real-time waveform.

**Why now?** Content Warning (2024) proved voice-reactive mechanics drive virality. 2026 TikTok rewards unusual input methods. No one has built a voice-controlled roguelite wave shooter — the closest is horror whisper games.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 6/10. Microphone input in Unity is well-supported but noisy; pitch detection libraries exist but have license considerations. The real risk is accessibility — mute, muted-while-streaming, and shared-living-situation players are locked out, so a keyboard fallback is required and doubles the input design work.

**Scope:** 6 months, including vocal-input tuning (which will dominate the last 6 weeks).

**Closest existing:** Rain World's voice detection modes; nothing in the shooter genre.

**Fatal flaw:** Players don't want to scream in their apartment at 11pm. If the game isn't playable at a whisper, the market shrinks to streamers. If it *is* playable at a whisper, the gimmick evaporates.

### 8. Deputy

**Pitch:** A wave shooter where you're the handler, not the gunner — you train a single AI partner during runs by rewarding its behavior, and watch it fight the next wave on its own.

**Core mechanic:** Between waves you deploy "reinforcement tokens" on actions the AI took last wave (killed a priority target → good; wasted ammo → bad). Over ~10 waves the partner's behavior tree visibly specializes. You still have a gun, but your real damage output is how well you've trained Deputy. Each run produces a differently-shaped AI — some runs end with a sniper partner, some with a reckless breacher.

**Why now?** AI-trainer games are a nascent sub-sub-genre (Astro Bot's companion, Rain World's slugcats). The 2026 cultural moment around AI alignment and "pet AI" is primed for a playful take. Nobody has shipped a wave-shooter version.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 4/10. Behavior-tree systems that *visibly change* based on player input are advanced AI work. Your Unity utility-AI knowledge matters more than your shooter chops here.

**Scope:** 12+ months. Honest answer: a solo dev with 1–2 years Unity should prototype this to validate the AI loop before committing — a 1-month ugly proof of concept is the gate.

**Closest existing:** Black & White (training creature, not shooter); nothing in the wave-shooter space.

**Fatal flaw:** If the AI doesn't *visibly* get smarter in a 20-minute run, the entire fantasy collapses. The AI design is the game, and under-delivering on it is a straight line to "clever idea, boring to play."
