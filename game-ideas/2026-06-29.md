# Game Concepts — 2026-06-29

For a solo Unity dev (1–2 yrs exp) currently building a wave survival shooter. Concepts are sized for that skill ceiling and lean toward shoot/defend/survive adjacency so existing systems can carry over.

## Market snapshot (what informed these)

- **Bullet Heaven** is now a formal Steam genre (May 2026). Pure clones are saturated; sub-genre niches — turn-based survivors, open-world survivors, desktop-roguelikes — are still wide open.
- **Meccha Chameleon** (Prop Hunt + painting) and **Shift At Midnight** (co-op horror + gas station sim) are the breakout viral indies of the month — both stitch a casual loop onto a hostile loop.
- Cultural waves to surf: World Cup 2026 memes, "Chinamaxxing" / RedNote crossover, KitKat Heist, Lana Del Rey × alligator marriage, Nihilistic Penguin, Food Jutsu (anime-summoning CapCut transition), home-café trend, analog hobby revival as digital-burnout response, House of the Dragon S3 (dragonback battles), The Bear final season, Olivia Rodrigo's June 12 album.
- Recurring solo-dev failure mode (confirmed in every dev retrospective this year): **scope**. One mechanic, one camera, one art pipeline.

## TREND-BASED (riding a current wave)

### 1. Home Café, Last Café
**Pitch:** Run a cozy late-night home café — until 11pm, when the regulars stop being regulars and you defend the espresso bar.

**Core mechanic:** First half of a run is cozy: pull shots, plate pastries, arrange the counter (drag-drop, no twitch). Each item you finish becomes a defensive tool when the night-shift wave starts — a steam wand is a flamethrower, a milk frother is a stun grenade, a croissant tray is a shield. Two loops, one inventory.

**Why now?** Home-café TikTok is everywhere in June 2026; Shift At Midnight proved cozy-sim-flips-to-horror has traction; nobody has bolted the cozy half onto a wave shooter yet.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 8/10 — both halves are grid-based, AI is simple (path-to-counter then path-to-player), art is stylized 2.5D so one artist's worth of assets stretches far.

**Scope:** 6 months for a tight single-shop loop with ~12 items and 10 night waves. 1 year for unlockable shops + multiple endings.

**Closest existing:** Shift At Midnight (co-op horror loop); Plate Up! (kitchen sim); Brotato (wave shooter).

**Fatal flaw:** The cozy and combat audiences barely overlap — risk of pleasing neither. Mitigation: lean cozy in marketing, let combat be the surprise.

---

### 2. Wyrmgullet
**Pitch:** Wave survival from the back of a dragon during the Battle of the Gullet — you don't fly it, you defend it.

**Core mechanic:** Mounted twin-stick shooter. The dragon flies a fixed arc through a naval battle each run; you rotate around its body shooting boarders, ballista bolts, and rival dragons climbing the chain. Dragon's HP is your HP. Between runs, equip the dragon (talon armor, breath modifications) à la a roguelike meta.

**Why now?** House of the Dragon S3 (the Battle of the Gullet itself) is the most-watched cable drama of summer 2026. Mounted-defense angle sidesteps the "I need to animate flight" problem.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 7/10 — dragon is a stationary platform from the camera's POV; the world moves past it. Animation budget collapses to one looped dragon-flight cycle.

**Scope:** 6 months if the dragon is a single static rig with parallax background; 1 year for multiple dragons + biomes.

**Closest existing:** Brotato (loop), Skies of Arcadia (vibe), Panzer Dragoon (camera).

**Fatal flaw:** GRRM IP-adjacent marketing is a legal minefield — rename and re-skin early. Don't even put "dragon" in the working title on socials.

---

### 3. Convoy: Kit Run
**Pitch:** Escort a stolen-candy convoy across a fictional Europe while paramilitary chocolate cartels try to take it back.

**Core mechanic:** You're a gunner on top of the lead truck. Truck drives itself along a procedural route; you rotate-and-fire on bikes, drones, helicopters. Truck damage is a meter — patch it between waves with cargo you sacrifice (lose chocolate → lose score, save chocolate → harder waves).

**Why now?** The €300k "KitKat Heist" was the meme of June; convoy-defense as a sub-genre died with Twisted Metal but the loop is timeless. Riding the meme buys 2 weeks of free marketing.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 8/10 — the existing wave shooter codebase ports cleanly. Truck is a moving spawn point; rest is the dev's current project with a camera change.

**Scope:** 3 months for a vertical slice (one route, 5 enemy types). 6 months for a real release.

**Closest existing:** Death Road to Canada (tone), Brotato (loop), Metal Slug (turret feel).

**Fatal flaw:** Meme half-life is ~6 weeks. Ship the demo by August or the cultural hook is gone — and then it's just a generic convoy shooter.

## GAP IN MARKET (nobody made this well yet)

### 4. Spell Brigade Local Co-op
**Pitch:** Two-player couch-co-op survivors-like where your spells fuse on overlap.

**Core mechanic:** Each player picks an element (fire/ice/lightning/earth). Standing in your partner's projectile zone *combines* spells — fire+ice = a freezing-then-shattering AoE, lightning+earth = a chained ground slam. Builds emerge from positioning, not from item pickups.

**Why now?** The bullet-heaven genre is saturated single-player but co-op is shockingly thin (Spell Brigade is online-only; Vampire Survivors co-op is a tacked-on mode). Local couch co-op in the genre is essentially empty. Steam Deck + handheld co-op is up 40% YoY.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 7/10 — two-character input is straightforward in Unity's Input System; the design challenge is balancing 16 fusion combos, which is content work not engineering work.

**Scope:** 6 months. The dev's existing wave-shooter code is already 60% of this game.

**Closest existing:** The Spell Brigade (online co-op only); Magicka (fusion mechanic, different genre).

**Fatal flaw:** Balancing 4×4 fusion combos around solo play (one player should still feel viable) is harder than it looks. Decision early: is solo a mode or a no?

### 5. Turn-Based Survivors
**Pitch:** Vampire Survivors, but it's chess. You move once per beat; so do they.

**Core mechanic:** Grid-based. Each turn you move 1 tile or fire. Weapons auto-target on a cadence (a whip fires every 3 turns, a fireball every 5). Enemies move 1 tile toward you per turn. Hundreds of them. The puzzle: read the next 3 turns ahead, position so your auto-attacks clear the path you need to escape into.

**Why now?** Vampire Crawlers (the official turn-based spinoff) launched to lukewarm reviews — the audience is hungry for a turn-based survivor that *isn't* lukewarm. Steam tag now exists; market is asking.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 9/10 — turn-based eliminates the hardest part of action wave shooters (frame-perfect collision, hitstop tuning, particle perf with 500 enemies). Sprites can be 2D pixel art; one artist-month for a full enemy roster.

**Scope:** 3 months for a roguelike-tight version (one biome, 8 weapons, 15 enemies). 6 months for a full release.

**Closest existing:** Vampire Crawlers (didn't stick the landing); Into the Breach (vibe of turn-based clarity).

**Fatal flaw:** Turn-based pacing kills the "I just got hit by 200 things at once" dopamine that defines the genre. Need a different feel goal — design for "Aha, I see the kill chain" instead of "Holy shit explosions."

### 6. Desktop Defender
**Pitch:** A bullet-heaven that runs as a desktop pet — defend your literal Windows desktop from invading icons.

**Core mechanic:** The game *is* a transparent always-on-top window. Enemies crawl out of your taskbar, your recycle bin, your unread emails (it reads the icon, not the contents). You auto-shoot. Between runs, your trash folder is your loot pool. Off-brand Clippy is a possible companion.

**Why now?** The "roguelike runs on your desktop" hook went viral when an off-brand Clippy version hit Steam in May. The space is wide open and tonally weird — perfect indie territory.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 6/10 — Unity can do transparent overlay windows on Windows but it's fiddly (HWND hacks); macOS is harder. Scope to Windows-only and the rating jumps to 8/10.

**Scope:** 6 months if Windows-only and you don't try to actually read filesystem contents (read icons + names only, no permissions surface).

**Closest existing:** Bonzi Buddy: The Roguelike (the viral Clippy thing); virtual-pet-meets-shooter is otherwise empty.

**Fatal flaw:** Antivirus software flags transparent always-on-top windows aggressively. Code-signing certificate ($300/yr) is non-optional. Also: the joke is a 20-minute joke unless the meta loop is genuinely fun.

## WILDCARD (experimental, swing for fences)

### 7. Stitch
**Pitch:** Wave survival where you knit your defenders in real-time using analog controller-wiggle inputs.

**Core mechanic:** Between waves, you "stitch" units using analog stick rotations — clockwise circles produce melee units, figure-eights produce ranged, etc. Faster/cleaner motions = stronger units. During waves, units auto-fight; you're back to circling sticks to reinforce. Two minds, one controller.

**Why now?** "Analog hobby" is the official anti-burnout aesthetic of 2026; knitting TikTok is a real and growing scene. Nobody has made a craft-as-game-mechanic shooter. Steam Deck's twin sticks are perfect for it.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 5/10 — the input system is bespoke (gesture recognition on analog input is solvable but takes a month to tune so it doesn't feel either too lenient or too punishing). Risk concentrated in a single feature.

**Scope:** 6 months if the gesture system works. 1 year if it needs two iterations.

**Closest existing:** Nothing in shooters. Closest spiritually: Black & White (gesture casting), Donut County (one-handed game-feel ownership).

**Fatal flaw:** If gesture detection is even 5% off, players will rage-quit and reviews will read "controls broken." This game is the gesture system; if it doesn't sing, nothing else matters.

---

### 8. Doppel Diner
**Pitch:** Four-player co-op wave shooter where one of you is secretly an alien doppelganger and the food is the win condition.

**Core mechanic:** Hybrid: cooperative wave defense in a diner (Shift At Midnight tone) + one randomly-assigned traitor whose objective is to poison plates without being caught. Honest players see customers attacking; the doppelganger sees teammates as the customers. Same scene, two truths.

**Why now?** Among Us-likes had a 4-year lull; the social-deduction itch is unscratched. Shift At Midnight made "diner = horror" viral. Co-op horror is having a moment (R.E.P.O., Lethal Company).

**Solo dev feasibility:** 4/10 — netcode is the killer. A solo dev doing real-time co-op + asymmetric info is signing up for 60% of their dev time being networking.

**Scope:** 1 year minimum. Realistically 18 months with a beta period.

**Closest existing:** Among Us (deduction), Lethal Company (co-op horror), Overcooked (kitchen pressure).

**Fatal flaw:** Online multiplayer as a solo dev's first networked project. Either use Photon/Mirror and accept the limits, or this scope is a project-killer. Strong recommendation: prototype as local-only first to prove the asymmetric-info loop, then decide on netcode.

---

## Recommendation if forced to pick one

**Concept #3 (Convoy: Kit Run)** for the existing wave-shooter codebase reuse and the 3-month vertical slice. **Concept #5 (Turn-Based Survivors)** if the dev wants a project where solo-dev constraints actually help the design rather than fight it.

Avoid #8 unless the dev wants to learn netcode as the actual goal of the next 18 months.
