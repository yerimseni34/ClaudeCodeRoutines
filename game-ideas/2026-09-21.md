# Game Concepts — 2026-09-21

Solo Unity dev, 1–2 years experience, currently building a wave survival shooter. Concepts below prioritize reuse of that skillset (wave logic, procedural upgrades, hordes/AI, arena/room-scale spaces) unless a wildcard explicitly departs from it.

Research signals used (Sept 2026):
- **Meccha Chameleon** (Prop Hunt + painting) went viral; weirdo co-op keeps outperforming projections.
- **Mewgenics** shipped huge; cat/absurd-animal branding still pulls.
- **Wikigacha** (JP) — collect real Wikipedia articles as gacha cards — went viral in Feb 2026.
- **Megabonk**, **Whisker Squadron: Survivor** prove solo-Unity VS-likes are still a live niche.
- "2026 is the new 2016" nostalgia wave across TikTok/X.
- Lana Del Rey × Jeremy Dufrene (alligator tour guide) marriage → wave of alligator/swamp memes.
- "Kinda Chic" meme format still going.
- Tornado Squirrel resurgence late Aug / early Sept.
- Cozy games, weirdo co-op, and exploration remain the most-cited underserved niches in indie market surveys.

## Trend-based (3)

### 1. Bayou Bride Bash
1. **Pitch:** Defend a chaotic Louisiana wedding from an escalating swarm of alligators while the ceremony insists on continuing.
2. **Core mechanic:** Top-down wave shooter around a fixed altar. The bride slowly walks the aisle on a fixed timer; each "vow" completed triggers a boss wave. Slain gators drop "blessings" (upgrades) that the priest reads aloud.
3. **Why now:** The Del Rey / alligator-tour-guide marriage kicked off a huge alligator meme wave in Sept 2026. Wedding-adjacent games (House Party, Wedding Witch) are having a moment. Meme is loud enough to earn a free wishlisting push if launched in the next 3–4 months.
4. **Feasibility: 9/10** — Directly reuses the wave shooter WIP. Fixed arena, one enemy type family, timer-based waves. Art is a stylized swamp — forgivable.
5. **Scope:** 3 months for a solid demo, 6 months for full release.
6. **Closest existing game:** Brotato / 20 Minutes Till Dawn, with a Kentucky Route Zero–adjacent aesthetic.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Meme half-life. If it ships in mid-2027 the joke is fossil. Ship the demo *this quarter* or don't ship it.

### 2. WikiSurvivor
1. **Pitch:** A Vampire Survivors-like where every run pulls five random Wikipedia articles and turns them into your character, weapons, and enemies.
2. **Core mechanic:** Auto-battle on procedural arenas. Article on "Bee" becomes a swarm weapon; "1919 World Series" becomes a boss faction; "Meringue" becomes a shield ability. Local LLM/rule-based parser assigns tags → mechanics.
3. **Why now:** Wikigacha's viral moment in JP showed people love "Wikipedia as content pipeline." VS-formula is still selling. Novelty hook + free content generation.
4. **Feasibility: 6/10** — Wave/AI is your wheelhouse. The design work (turning arbitrary articles into balanced mechanics) is the real cost, plus edge-case moderation.
5. **Scope:** 6–9 months. Not 3 — the article-to-mechanic mapping alone is a month's design work.
6. **Closest existing game:** Vampire Survivors × Wikigacha × Peglin.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Moderation. Sooner or later a run pulls an article about a real tragedy or living person and gamifies it. Have a blocklist + curated subset ready or don't ship.

### 3. Y2K16
1. **Pitch:** A wave shooter set in a haunted 2016 shopping mall, mumble-rap on the food-court radio, Snapchat filters as power-ups.
2. **Core mechanic:** Room-by-room wave clears in a decaying mall. Currency is likes; upgrades are early-Instagram filters ("Valencia = piercing damage"). Boss is a Pokémon Go lure that summoned something it shouldn't have.
3. **Why now:** "2026 is the new 2016" nostalgia wave is loud on TikTok/X. Late-2010s aesthetic isn't yet as saturated as PS1/Y2K nostalgia.
4. **Feasibility: 9/10** — Reskin of your current WIP. Mall arenas are boxy → cheap to model. Music/aesthetic carries.
5. **Scope:** 3 months.
6. **Closest existing game:** Cruelty Squad meets Perfect Dark Sourcemod meets any liminal-mall walker.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Nostalgia window is narrow and PS1/Y2K devs will pivot into 2010s the moment they smell blood. First-mover only.

## Gap-in-market (3)

### 4. Wash Cycle
1. **Pitch:** Run a 24-hour laundromat by day, defend it from junkies and vending-machine ghouls at night — same building, two loops.
2. **Core mechanic:** Day loop is a cozy small-business sim (fold, sort, restock, chat). Night loop is a wave shooter using the *exact same* laundromat — dryers become cover, quarters become currency for both loops.
3. **Why now:** "Small business sim" is a documented underserved niche (per Fungies/Accio market reports), and "single space, day/night duality" is a proven design (Moonlighter, Graveyard Keeper). Nobody has done it cozy-shop + wave-defense specifically.
4. **Feasibility: 7/10** — The wave half is what you do. The sim half is one machine loop plus dialogue trees. Same building = shared assets.
5. **Scope:** 6 months.
6. **Closest existing game:** Moonlighter (structure), Punch Club (vibe), Coffee Talk (day loop).
7. **Fatal flaw:** Cozy-sim audience and wave-shooter audience barely overlap. You may build two half-games that split marketing rather than reinforce each other. Pick a lead genre and market to *that* audience only.

### 5. Metro Guardian
1. **Pitch:** Mini Metro if the tunnels also spawned things and you had to kill them before the 8:15 arrived.
2. **Core mechanic:** Draw and manage subway lines like Mini Metro; each new station triggers a wave from that tunnel that a small automated crew must clear before the next train. You allocate crew, weapons, and upgrades between stations.
3. **Why now:** Transit sims have no recent hit (Mini Metro is 2016; NIMBY Rails is niche). Post-pandemic urbanism content is TikTok-viral. No existing wave-defense × transit hybrid.
4. **Feasibility: 5/10** — Pathfinding + tycoon UI + wave AI is three systems, and the UI is the killer. Not a beginner-Unity project.
5. **Scope:** 12 months, honestly. Don't lie to yourself.
6. **Closest existing game:** Mini Metro + They Are Billions.
7. **Fatal flaw:** UI/UX debt. Transit sims live or die on line-drawing feel. If you can't nail that in the first month, kill the project.

### 6. Field Trip Overnight
1. **Pitch:** A class of eight-year-olds gets locked in the natural history museum overnight. You're the chaperone. The exhibits are waking up.
2. **Core mechanic:** 1–4 player co-op. One "chaperone" per player, ~15 wandering kid NPCs. You herd kids toward safe rooms while defending against animated exhibits. Kids drop backpacks (upgrades). Losing a kid ends the run.
3. **Why now:** Weirdo co-op (Lethal Company, Content Warning, R.E.P.O., Meccha Chameleon) is the loudest emerging category in 2026. Cozy horror is under-explored. Museum setting is asset-cheap (a few room templates).
4. **Feasibility: 6/10** — Netcode is where solo Unity devs die. Consider offline co-op or Steam Remote Play Together as an out.
5. **Scope:** 6–9 months if you use Steamworks + Mirror or Photon Fusion; 12+ if you roll your own.
6. **Closest existing game:** Lethal Company × Overcooked × Night at the Museum.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Netcode. Second failure mode: escort-AI (the kids) is one of the hardest AI problems in games. Prototype the kid pathfinding in week one.

## Wildcards (2)

### 7. Tornado Squirrel
1. **Pitch:** You are a squirrel in a tornado. You cannot leave the tornado. Everything else is negotiable.
2. **Core mechanic:** Physics roguelike. The tornado is a constant upward force; you steer left/right within it. Debris flies past — acorns heal, fridges kill, chainsaws attach as weapons. Each run ends when the tornado dies out or you fall out the bottom.
3. **Why now:** Tornado Squirrel meme resurged late Aug / early Sept 2026. Physics-comedy games (Human Fall Flat, Totally Accurate Battle Simulator) still print. Auto-forward-motion roguelikes (Downwell, HoloCure) are proven.
4. **Feasibility: 8/10** — Unity physics + spawn tables. One-screen game. Very few animations needed.
5. **Scope:** 3–6 months.
6. **Closest existing game:** Downwell in a blender with Peggle and Vampire Survivors.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Single-joke game. Steam page has to sell the fantasy in one gif or it's dead. If the gif isn't obviously funny, the game isn't obviously funny.

### 8. One Candle
1. **Pitch:** Defend a single lit candle in a single procedural room from an escalating night of visitors who want it out.
2. **Core mechanic:** The entire game takes place in one 6×6m room. You are a small spirit with no body, moving lightweight objects (a broom, a mug, a book) via telekinesis to obstruct/attack intruders. Each wave changes room contents; between waves you spend "wick" to buy new furniture that becomes both cover and weapon.
3. **Why now:** Design-constraint games (Downwell's single column, Vampire Survivors' single-room feel, Luck be a Landlord's single machine) keep finding audiences. Cozy-horror aesthetic underserved.
4. **Feasibility: 9/10** — Bounded scope. One room, one lighting rig, procedural furniture layout. Perfect solo scope.
5. **Scope:** 3 months for demo, 5 for full release.
6. **Closest existing game:** 20 Minutes Till Dawn × Luck be a Landlord × Little Nightmares.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Risk of feeling shallow after 30 minutes. Needs a strong meta-progression / narrative frame (who lit the candle? why does it matter?) or players bounce. Write the frame *before* the code.

---

## Recommendation

If you want the shortest path from your current WIP to a shippable, marketable product: **#1 (Bayou Bride Bash)** or **#3 (Y2K16)** — both are essentially reskins of what you're already building, ride current cultural signal, and can plausibly demo in ~90 days. **#8 (One Candle)** is the wildcard I'd actually bet on for longevity — it's the most disciplined scope with the highest chance of a durable design.

Avoid **#5 (Metro Guardian)** and **#6 (Field Trip Overnight)** on this dev's experience level unless the timeline explicitly allows a year.
