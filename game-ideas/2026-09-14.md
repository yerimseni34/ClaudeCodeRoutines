# Game Concepts — 2026-09-14

Ten wave-survival-shooter-adjacent concepts for a solo Unity dev (1–2 years experience). Mix of trend-riding, market-gap, and wildcard ideas. Each concept is written to be honest about scope and risk — the fatal flaws are the point.

**Context on the market (as of Sept 2026):** Steam officially recognized "Bullet Heaven" as a genre tag in May 2026. The cosmetic-only Vampire Survivors clone wave has receded — 2026's commercial winners bring mechanical innovation or a real premise. Sub-genres like open-world survivors-likes and spellslinger survivors-likes are fragmenting off. That means: a plain wave shooter no longer sells itself, but a wave shooter with one distinctive twist has more room than ever.

Cultural moments referenced below: the GTA 6 hacker-leak saga ("Cyberleek"), the Harry Potter TV series and Avengers: Doomsday hype cycles, *One Battle After Another*'s Oscar sweep, and the "Kinda Chic" / "Say Wallahi" / "City Boy" meme wave.

---

## 1. LEAKED.EXE — Trend

**Pitch:** You are Cyberleek, a hacker inside a AAA studio's dev build, and every wave is a corporate security patch trying to delete you before you can exfiltrate the next asset.

**Core mechanic:** Top-down twin-stick shooter where "bullets" are decompiled code fragments. Between waves you *choose which leaked asset to keep* — each one is a permanent modifier (a vehicle physics module gives dashes; a weather system gives an AoE storm; an NPC dialogue tree gives a summonable ally). Waves escalate as the studio's anti-cheat wakes up.

**Why now?** The GTA 6 leak/Cyberleek saga has been the single biggest gaming-news story of 2026. The "hacker vs. corporation" fantasy is culturally hot and legally interesting without needing to touch a real IP.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 7/10. Twin-stick shooters are a well-trodden Unity path. The asset-as-upgrade metaphor is a clean reskin of a standard perk system; art can lean glitch/wireframe/ASCII which is cheap.

**Scope:** 6 months for a tight, replayable version. 3 months only if you cut the meta-progression and ship a pure arcade loop.

**Closest existing game:** *Nuclear Throne* meets *Vampire Survivors*, with a *Hypnospace Outlaw* aesthetic layer.

**Fatal flaw:** The theme is downstream of a real, ongoing legal saga. Publishers and Steam may pattern-match it as trouble; the joke also ages the moment the leak story does.

## 2. WANDCRAFT — Trend

**Pitch:** A spellslinger survivors-like where you don't pick spells from a menu — you *draw them* with the mouse, and the shape you draw determines the effect.

**Core mechanic:** Wave survival with no weapon slots. Every 8 seconds you can trace a glyph; a circle is AoE, a line is a beam, a zigzag is chain-lightning, a spiral is a homing swarm. Combining glyphs mid-wave produces compound effects. Meta-progression unlocks new elemental "inks."

**Why now?** The Harry Potter TV series is dominating fantasy discourse through 2026, and "spellslinger survivors-like" is being called out by name as an emerging sub-genre. The gesture-drawing spell input is a decade-old idea (Arx Fatalis, Black & White) that no survivors-like has claimed yet.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 6/10. Gesture recognition is a real engineering task — you'll want `$P` or `$1` recognizers, which are well-documented but need careful tuning to feel good on trackpads and controllers.

**Scope:** 9–12 months. The gesture layer eats time; balancing 15+ glyphs with combinatorial effects is the long tail.

**Closest existing game:** *Magicka* × *Vampire Survivors*, or *Noita* if Noita had legible progression.

**Fatal flaw:** Gesture input on Steam Deck / controller is genuinely hard to get right. If it feels janky in the first 30 seconds, no one refunds patiently.

## 3. GUERRILLA — Trend

**Pitch:** A wave survival shooter about defending a hidden safehouse from waves of increasingly militarized authorities, inspired by *One Battle After Another*'s revolution-in-hiding tone.

**Core mechanic:** You don't fight in the open. Between waves you rearrange furniture, board windows, hide contraband, and place traps in a single procedural apartment. During a wave, enemies breach and you fight room-to-room with a limited magazine — reloading takes 3 seconds and you feel every one. Losing a room permanently reduces safe hiding space for the next wave.

**Why now?** *One Battle After Another* swept the Oscars and the "revolution as domestic life" theme is having a moment. Nobody's shipped a wave shooter that is actually about the *domestic* geometry of resistance.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 7/10. Constrained arena (one apartment) keeps art scope small. Enemy AI needs door/window logic but not much else.

**Scope:** 6 months for one procedural apartment layout system + 20 waves. A full campaign with named characters is a year.

**Closest existing game:** *Beholder* × *Door Kickers* × *They Are Billions*, played from the wrong side.

**Fatal flaw:** The political frame will polarize half the audience before they read the store page. Also, "reload takes 3 seconds and it matters" is either your signature or the reason people bounce.

## 4. HOMESTEAD.44 — Gap

**Pitch:** A wave shooter where the base you build between waves *stays permanently on the map* across the entire run — no more disposable "meta upgrades," the arena itself is your progress bar.

**Core mechanic:** Top-down survival. Kill waves, collect scrap, place walls / turrets / crops / power lines during a 60-second build phase. The map is a single 200×200 tile grid you never leave. By wave 30 the arena is your fortress; by wave 50 you're defending it against enemies that can dig, climb, or teleport.

**Why now?** Every survivors-like has "meta upgrades," but almost none of them make the *arena itself* the persistent artifact. Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor and its ilk still wipe the map every run. There is a real gap between "wave shooter" and "base builder" that no solo dev has cleanly bridged.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 6/10. The build system is the risk — grid placement, pathfinding for enemies around your walls, and save/load of arbitrary map state all need to be solid.

**Scope:** 9 months. The vertical slice (build + defend one wave) is ~6 weeks; the long tail is enemy variety that stresses your build system in new ways.

**Closest existing game:** *They Are Billions* compressed, or *Dome Keeper* with real terrain.

**Fatal flaw:** Pathfinding around player-built walls at scale is a performance and design tarpit. If a player builds a maze, does the AI break? If the AI ignores it, why did they build?

## 5. SWORDS ONLY — Gap

**Pitch:** A first-person melee-only wave survival game — the bullet-heaven power fantasy, but every kill is a hit you swung yourself.

**Core mechanic:** FPS view. No guns. You pick a weapon (katana, halberd, chain-whip, boxing gloves) with distinct reach, arc, and stamina cost. Enemies come in Vampire-Survivors-density waves, but you clear them by physically sweeping with the mouse. Meta upgrades add cleave, ricochet, and reactive effects (a strike lights the ground on fire, etc.) so late-run you're one-shot-clearing screens with a single arc.

**Why now?** Every VS-like is ranged. The "melee bullet heaven" is a genuinely open niche and *Midnight Horde* (mid-2026) proved the audience wants a shape they haven't seen. First-person melee tech has quietly gotten easy in Unity (Blade & Sorcery has trained a decade of asset store devs).

**Solo dev feasibility:** 8/10. Small enemy AI, no ranged combat logic, arena can be a single room. Melee juice (screenshake, hit-stop, decapitation particles) is well-documented territory.

**Scope:** 3–6 months. A vertical slice is genuinely achievable in a weekend jam. The whole game in 3 months is possible if you commit to one arena, one weapon at launch, and lean into replay via modifiers.

**Closest existing game:** *Boneworks* × *Vampire Survivors*, minus the VR.

**Fatal flaw:** FPS melee that doesn't feel weighty is worse than a bad shooter. If the hits don't land right in the first 10 seconds, you're done — and "feel" is the least schedulable part of gamedev.

## 6. DEMOLITION DERBY — Gap

**Pitch:** A wave shooter where the arena is fully destructible and *never regenerates*, so by wave 20 you're fighting in a bombed-out crater of your own making.

**Core mechanic:** Top-down shooter in a voxel or 2D-destructible arena. Every bullet, explosion, and enemy corpse deforms terrain. Cover you shot through in wave 3 is gone in wave 10. You have to *choose* whether to fight defensively (preserve cover) or aggressively (level the place and hope you outrun them).

**Why now?** Real-time destruction was expensive in 2018; in 2026 it's a Unity asset-store checkbox (see the RayFire / Destructible 2D ecosystems). No shooter has made destruction *the run-defining variable* rather than a graphical flourish.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 6/10. The destruction tech is off-the-shelf, but *balancing* around it — making sure the run doesn't degenerate into "flat plain full of enemies" — is a real design problem.

**Scope:** 6 months. Vertical slice in 8 weeks; the rest is enemy variety that interacts with terrain (burrowers, climbers, ranged behind cover).

**Closest existing game:** *Teardown* × *Brotato*, or a top-down *Rain World*.

**Fatal flaw:** Save/load of destruction state is a memory nightmare, and once the arena flattens, every wave looks the same. The mid-run "everything is rubble" phase must feel *different*, not just uglier.

## 7. LATE SHIFT — Gap

**Pitch:** A wave survival shooter set in a single 24-hour convenience store, where every wave is a different kind of customer — real customers, shoplifters, corporate auditors, and eventually the supernatural.

**Core mechanic:** First-person, single-location. You stock shelves, work the register, and defend the store from escalating threats. Early waves are literal customer service (scan items on a timer); mid waves are shoplifters you tackle; late waves are hordes of eldritch things climbing over the frozen-food aisle. Money earned in "normal" shifts buys weapons for the "abnormal" ones.

**Why now?** The "liminal retail space" aesthetic (backrooms, dead malls, night shift horror) is a durable meme and the "Kinda Chic" trend has extended its shelf life. A store-defense shooter has been requested repeatedly on r/gamedev's monthly "wish this existed" threads.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 8/10. One indoor environment. Small pool of enemy types. The tonal shift from mundane to eldritch is doing most of the work — you don't need huge art scope.

**Scope:** 6 months, comfortably. A short-form version could ship in 3.

**Closest existing game:** *Night Shift at Freddy's* × *Vampire Survivors*, with *Supermarket Simulator* DNA in the calm phase.

**Fatal flaw:** The "boring on purpose" opening is a huge trailer problem. If a streamer plays the first 5 minutes on camera and it looks like a job simulator, you lose the audience before the horror arrives.

## 8. CITY BOY — Trend / Wildcard

**Pitch:** A rhythm-based wave shooter where you fight to the beat of your own on-the-fly-composed lo-fi track, riding the "City Boy" and "Kinda Chic" aesthetic waves.

**Core mechanic:** Isometric shooter. You have four "instrument" weapons (kick, snare, bass, lead). Firing on the beat does 2× damage and layers that instrument into an evolving track. Miss the beat and the layer drops out. Late-run you're playing an actual composition to survive; the soundtrack of your best run is exportable as an MP3.

**Why now?** The "City Boy" and "Kinda Chic" meme aesthetics are peaking on TikTok. Lo-fi rhythm games (BPM, Metronomicon) are cult favorites but haven't intersected cleanly with the survivors-like boom.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 5/10. Beat detection and music-driven procedural generation are their own engineering domain. The music itself is the biggest ask — you either compose it or license loops, both non-trivial.

**Scope:** 12 months, honestly. Rhythm games are notorious for having "one more polish pass" indefinitely.

**Closest existing game:** *BPM: Bullets Per Minute* × *Brotato*, with an *Everhood* vibe.

**Fatal flaw:** Rhythm games with even ~30ms latency drift feel broken. You'll spend a month just on audio-visual sync alone before the game is playable.

## 9. GHOSTED — Wildcard

**Pitch:** A wave shooter where every enemy is a recording of your own past runs — you are fighting yourself, and every run makes the next one harder.

**Core mechanic:** Standard top-down survival shooter — but every run records your movement, shots, and death location. Next run, one enemy in wave 1 is a ghost of your previous character. By run 20, half the horde is you. Every death makes the game measurably more dangerous, forever, unless you spend a rare "exorcism" item.

**Why now?** Asynchronous multiplayer (Elden Ring's messages, Death Stranding's structures) is having a moment as a "warmer" multiplayer. Nobody has applied it to wave survival, and it solves the survivors-like's biggest late-game problem: eventually you outgrow the enemies.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 7/10. Recording player input is cheap. Replaying it as enemy behavior is cheap. The tech is basically free; the design is where it earns its keep.

**Scope:** 6 months. Vertical slice in a month.

**Closest existing game:** *Trackmania*'s ghost mechanic × *Vampire Survivors*, with a hint of *Undertale*'s memory-mechanic vibe.

**Fatal flaw:** If the ghost AI feels dumb (players don't move like a good horde) the fantasy collapses. And "the game gets harder every death, forever" is a hard sell to a mainstream audience — the framing has to be a *feature*, not a punishment.

## 10. GARDEN DEFENSE — Wildcard

**Pitch:** A cozy, non-violent wave survival game where you defend your garden from waves of friendly-but-hungry creatures using scarecrows, sprinklers, and whistles instead of weapons.

**Core mechanic:** Top-down. Waves of rabbits, deer, raccoons, and eventually mythological beasts (a well-mannered dragon) enter your garden and start eating. You place non-lethal deterrents — scarecrows have range and rotation, sprinklers do AoE, wind chimes debuff. If a creature eats a plant, you lose that plant; if all plants are eaten, the wave is "lost" but you don't die, you just start next year with less. It's *Stardew Valley*'s cozy loop grafted onto a wave shooter skeleton.

**Why now?** The "cozy games" category is enormous and still underserved on the *action* side — almost every cozy game is turn-based or unstructured. A cozy game with real-time tension is a genuine gap. It also sidesteps the entire "violence in games" conversation, which has quietly become a market segment.

**Solo dev feasibility:** 9/10. No combat animations. No blood, no ragdolls, no player death handling. The whole game is placement + timers + cute sprites. The most Unity-friendly project on this list.

**Scope:** 3 months for a genuine vertical release. 6 months for a full seasonal / weather / creature-variety version that could headline a Nintendo Direct-style indie showcase.

**Closest existing game:** *Plants vs. Zombies* × *Stardew Valley*, minus the zombies.

**Fatal flaw:** Cozy audiences are famously ad-averse and stream-averse, so discovery is hard. And "wave shooter, but with no shooting" may confuse the survivors-like audience your studio's skills point toward.

## Recommendations for this dev

Given "solo, Unity, 1–2 years, wave survival shooter" as the constraint set:

- **Fastest to ship:** #5 (Swords Only) or #10 (Garden Defense). Both are 3-month achievable, both have distinctive hooks, both reuse skills you already have.
- **Highest ceiling:** #4 (Homestead.44) or #9 (Ghosted). Both take the survivors-like formula and give it a *durable* meta that solves the genre's replay problem, which is where the money is in 2026.
- **Trend-timed:** #1 (LEAKED.EXE) is culturally the hottest but has the shortest shelf life — if you can't ship inside 6 months, don't start.
- **Avoid unless you have collaborators:** #8 (City Boy). Rhythm games are the classic solo-dev graveyard.
