# Game Concepts — 2026-10-05

Target: solo indie dev, 1-2 years Unity, currently building a wave survival shooter.
Lens: lean into existing survivors-genre muscle while scouting adjacent niches, cultural tailwinds, and shippable scopes.

**Market snapshot going in**
- Survivors-likes (Vampire Survivors, Brotato, Megabonk) are still a volume genre; Megabonk showed a crude-looking 3D auto-shooter from a first-time dev can hit 20k concurrent in a week. Formula is proven but **saturated** — differentiation matters more than ever.
- Fall 2026 cultural weather: Pumpkin Head Illusion (TikTok), the "6 7"/Doot Doot audio meme, Ramalama Costume Walk, "Two Fishes" audio, "Be OK" audio, pumpkin spice/fall-core season.
- Streaming heat: *Lanterns* (HBO, 4-week #1), *Coven Academy* (Disney+), *Monster: The Lizzie Borden Story* (Netflix true-crime), *Ted Lasso S4* (Apple).
- Niche opportunities per Steam 2026 analysis: **job simulators** (34.7% chance of 1k reviews, improving success rate), **narrative-driven**, **cozy**, **exploration** — all underserved relative to interest.

## 1. Pumpkin Patch Panic — *trend-based*

**Pitch.** A seasonal horror wave-survival roguelite where you're a scarecrow with a pumpkin head defending a cul-de-sac from everything spilling out of the suburb's cursed Halloween decorations.

**Core mechanic.** Twin-stick auto-fire using "candy" currency dropped by hordes to re-light your pumpkin (which doubles as HP + light radius). Dark areas spawn stronger enemies; replacing your pumpkin on jack-o'-lantern pedestals around the yard expands your safe zone.

**Why now.** TikTok's Pumpkin Head Illusion trend is actively viral, pumpkin spice/fall-core is at peak visibility, and horror-adjacent survivors drops perform predictably well in October-November. Ships in time to ride *next* October if six-month scope.

**Solo dev feasibility.** 9/10. Core loop is a reskin of the shooter you already know, with top-down 2D pixel/vector art. Lighting-as-mechanic is cheap in URP 2D.

**Scope.** 3 months to a Steam demo, 6 months to EA.

**Closest existing.** *Brotato* × *Vampire Survivors*, with the lantern meta of *Darkest Dungeon*.

**Fatal flaw.** Seasonal games have a short tail — if launched outside Sept-Nov window, discoverability drops a cliff. Also competes directly with every other spooky survivors clone this fall.

## 2. Coven Semester — *trend-based*

**Pitch.** You're a first-year at a witch academy whose dorm is besieged nightly by what the seniors unleashed; each night you survive, each day you pick classes that reshape your spell loadout.

**Core mechanic.** Wave survival at night (auto-cast spells with combo system: fire + wind = firestorm). Daytime is a 10-minute "schedule builder" where you drag spells into class slots to level them, with social-sim-lite relationships that unlock co-cast spells. One run = one semester.

**Why now.** *Coven Academy* is the #1 Disney+ title; witch-coded media (*Agatha All Along* holdover, Studio Ghibli revival) has been durable for 24 months. Students-at-magic-school is the single most reliably bankable Western fantasy frame right now.

**Solo dev feasibility.** 7/10. Day/night split adds a second system to design and tune. Dialogue is cheap if kept text-only; art is the risk.

**Scope.** 6-9 months. 1 year if voice-acted.

**Closest existing.** *Hogwarts Legacy* (narrative frame) × *20 Minutes Till Dawn* (loop) × *Persona* (schedule loop).

**Fatal flaw.** Licensing panic — if "witch academy" reads as Hogwarts to Steam reviewers, you inherit discourse you don't want. Stylize *hard* away from British boarding school.

## 3. Lantern Keepers — *trend-based*

**Pitch.** A co-op (and solo) wave defender set in a drowned city where lamplighters hold back the dark with networked lanterns; every lantern you light is a turret, every lantern that goes out is one less you have.

**Core mechanic.** You run a route through a hub lighting lanterns on a timer; each lit lantern auto-attacks (your "weapons" are placements), oil is the economy, and enemies specifically hunt and extinguish lanterns, forcing you to defend infrastructure, not just yourself.

**Why now.** *Lanterns* has been the #1 HBO show for four weeks running — "lantern" is in audience vocabulary. The oil/lamplight aesthetic also laps up Victorian-horror and *Dishonored*-core nostalgia.

**Solo dev feasibility.** 7/10. Place-and-defend is a well-understood pattern; co-op networking is the main scope risk (defer to post-launch or use a lockstep lobby lib).

**Scope.** 6 months solo. 9 months with co-op.

**Closest existing.** *They Are Billions* × *Dome Keeper* × a top-down *BioShock Infinite*.

**Fatal flaw.** "Defend the lanterns" is a *loss-conditioned* loop — players feel punished rather than empowered unless you make lantern deaths feel like a *choice*, not a failure.

## 4. Night Shift — *gap in market*

**Pitch.** A horror wave-survival sim where you're the sole night janitor in a 24-floor haunted office tower and your weapons are the cleaning cart you'd actually have.

**Core mechanic.** Mop handles become spears, floor-waxer becomes AoE, vacuum becomes a slow-field — ordinary custodial tools you *upgrade* (not swap). Core objective is to *finish the cleaning checklist* (empty bins, mop floor, vacuum, lock up) while hordes spawn from paranormal clutter you leave behind. Dirty rooms spawn more enemies, so maintenance **is** combat.

**Why now.** Job simulators are the single improving indie success category on Steam, and horror-job hybrids (Content Warning, Lethal Company) proved blue-collar + occult has mass pull. Nobody's done *janitor* seriously.

**Solo dev feasibility.** 9/10. Looping office floors are the cheapest level art in gamedev (modular, repetitive, intentionally fluorescent-ugly). You already own the wave code.

**Scope.** 4-5 months.

**Closest existing.** *Viscera Cleanup Detail* × *Lethal Company* × *Vampire Survivors*.

**Fatal flaw.** Janitorial loops can skew *tedious* — if cleaning becomes busywork rather than tactic, the "job" eats the "survival" and nobody finishes the demo.

## 5. The Last Barista — *gap in market*

**Pitch.** A cozy wave-survival shopkeeping game where the apocalypse is outside, you run the only espresso bar left, and your clientele are the same drifters who defend the perimeter.

**Core mechanic.** Between waves: brew drinks on a timing minigame; each drink buffs the drifter who orders it, who then fights for you in the next wave. Different beans/milks/syrups = different buff trees. You never fire a gun — your "shooter" is who you choose to caffeinate.

**Why now.** Cozy + survival is the single most underserved combination in indie right now; shopkeep sims (*Potionomics*, *Travellers Rest*, *Probably Stolen* coming Oct 29) are performing. The "sincere coffee shop" aesthetic maps directly onto the pumpkin-spice / fall-core audience.

**Solo dev feasibility.** 8/10. One room, no real combat animation to make yourself — the drifters do the fighting off-camera or as auto-battler sprites.

**Scope.** 5-6 months.

**Closest existing.** *Coffee Talk* × *Kingdom Two Crowns* × *Dave the Diver*'s restaurant loop.

**Fatal flaw.** Vicarious combat is a hard sell to shooter players, and cozy players may bounce the moment they see waves. Marketing audience is narrow — the Venn overlap you need might not actually exist.

## 6. Zoo Breach — *gap in market*

**Pitch.** A non-lethal wave-defense game where the zoo containment just failed and you, the night shift zookeeper, have to tranq-dart escaped animals back into their enclosures before dawn tours arrive.

**Core mechanic.** Twin-stick with tranq-rifle (ammo = finite, reload is slow), with secondary tools (nets, treat-lures, keeper call). Each animal species has a *behavior profile* — not an HP bar — so lions chase you if you run, elephants ignore darts but respond to lures, octopi escape pens you re-close. Shift ends at sunrise; count re-contained vs. escaped.

**Why now.** Zero recent entries in sincere animal-care-under-pressure. *Planet Zoo* is placid; *Zoo Tycoon* is extinct. Non-lethal shooters are a real underserved sub-genre (*Pokémon Snap*, *Beast Breaker*).

**Solo dev feasibility.** 7/10. Animal AI is the real cost — each species needs a distinct behavior, and that doesn't scale. Mitigate with a *small* roster (6-8 animals) handled deeply.

**Scope.** 6-8 months.

**Closest existing.** *Zookeeper Simulator* × *Peace, Death!* × behavior-forward *Dishonored*.

**Fatal flaw.** If the darts feel like regular bullets with a reskin, it's just a shooter. The *behavioral AI* is the whole product — if that's shallow, nothing else works.

## 7. 6 7 — *wildcard*

**Pitch.** A rhythm-locked wave shooter where every weapon fires only on the "6" and the "7" of a 6/7 time signature and the meme audio is the whole soundtrack.

**Core mechanic.** You auto-walk; firing is locked to beats 6 and 7 of each measure, and the HUD is a bouncing "6 7" indicator. Perfect timing chains = combo meter = screen-clear specials. Enemies spawn *in rhythm*, so reading the beat *is* reading the battlefield.

**Why now.** The "6 7" / Doot Doot audio is in Wikipedia-tier cultural saturation; it's also the kind of meme that *resents* commercial exploitation, so a free demo is a near-mandatory first move. Rhythm-roguelikes (*BPM*, *Metal: Hellsinger*, *Rift of the NecroDancer*) have shown this hybrid monetizes.

**Solo dev feasibility.** 6/10. Rhythm-game feel is a brutal polish discipline; latency calibration alone eats a sprint. But scope is otherwise tight.

**Scope.** 4-6 months for a one-track proof-of-concept; 1 year for a full soundtrack.

**Closest existing.** *Soundfall* × *Vampire Survivors*, with *BPM*'s rhythm-gating.

**Fatal flaw.** Meme half-life. If "6 7" is dead by ship, the whole brand is dust. Build so the game stands on rhythm mechanics even if you had to strip the meme audio tomorrow.

## 8. Two Fishes — *wildcard*

**Pitch.** An auto-battler where you're on a dating show defending a take nobody asked you to defend, and every bad opinion you back summons another argument that stalks you across the villa.

**Core mechanic.** Pre-wave, you pick a hot take from a menu ("you don't have to put one back"). Each wave, enemies spawn themed to counterarguments (bad-faith hecklers, concern trolls, sincere disagreers), and your weapons are *rhetorical devices* (deflect, pivot, straw-man, concede). Winning a wave *doubles down*; losing one *clarifies* (makes your take saner but weaker).

**Why now.** "Two Fishes" is a current Love Island-adjacent TikTok format with legs because the premise ("defending a take you shouldn't have to") is evergreen. Satirical mechanics are a *moat* no AAA will touch.

**Solo dev feasibility.** 6/10. Writing load is the real cost — you need witty variants for each take × each counterargument. Mechanics are small; words are the project.

**Scope.** 5-7 months with a tight writing scope (10 takes, 4 counter-types = 40 scripted encounters).

**Closest existing.** *Suzerain* (rhetoric-as-mechanic) × *20 Minutes Till Dawn* × *Thank Goodness You're Here*'s tone.

**Fatal flaw.** Tone is one slipped joke from mean-spirited. If the game *punches* its subjects instead of punching alongside them, it reads as cruel and loses the audience that would've loved it most.
