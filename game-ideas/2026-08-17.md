# Game Concept Brainstorm — 2026-08-17

Context: solo dev, 1–2 yrs Unity, currently working in the wave-survival-shooter
space. Concepts biased toward mechanics that a single person can actually ship,
with hooks that a Twitter clip or TikTok can carry.

## What the market looks like this week

- **Megabonk** just crossed 1M copies in two weeks and beat *Hades II*'s all-time
  concurrent Steam record — a 3D reverse-bullet-hell from one dev, sold at $10,
  carried by streamer memes. The "3D twist on Vampire Survivors" thesis is still
  paying, but the direct-clone lane is choking on itself: dozens of $5 Survivors
  clones are shipping to <500 players.
- **Cultural wave** (Aug 2026): Labubu blind-box obsession is still climbing;
  "Italian brainrot" AI-hybrid animals (Bombardiro Crocodilo etc.) dominate the
  meme layer; "Six Seven" TikTok dance is the audio meme of the summer; a 90s
  minimalist / old-money aesthetic is running counter-programming to the chaos.
- **Underserved lanes** flagged by press: 2D side-scrollers, couch co-op,
  short-loop arcade revivals. Vampire-Survivors-likes are saturated at the top
  and empty in every adjacent shape.
- **Solo-dev reality**: the winners this year (Megabonk, PEAK, the Brotato tail)
  all shipped one clean mechanic with a strong 10-second visual read. Nobody
  ships a systems-heavy 6-mechanic game in a year alone.

Below: 3 trend-based, 3 gap-in-market, 2 wildcard. Each rated for a solo dev
with the constraints above.

---

## TREND-BASED (riding a current wave)

### 1. Blind Box Survivor

**Pitch.** A wave-survival roguelike where every level-up drops a *sealed
mystery box* you have to physically shake, tear, and unbox for your next
weapon — turning the biggest ritual on TikTok right now into the game's core
loop.

- **Core mechanic.** Standard auto-attack wave survival, but XP → boxes, not
  perk-picks. Each box has a rarity, a shake weight, and 2–4 hidden contents;
  you decide whether to open now (fast, safe pick) or hold and combine boxes
  for guaranteed rarity tiers. Duplicates fuse into a display shelf that gives
  meta-run bonuses — so "pulls" persist between runs like a real collection.
- **Why now?** Labubu / Pop Mart blind-box unboxings are getting 5M-view
  weekends on TikTok; the ritual translates 1:1 to a game verb. No survivors
  game has made *opening* the loot the fun part yet — it's always instant.
- **Solo dev feasibility.** **8/10.** Reuses standard wave-shooter architecture
  (spawner + upgrade pool). New work is the box system, the shake/tear
  animation feel, and the collection shelf UI. Art can lean toy-render / vinyl
  figure — one shader, forgiving topology.
- **Scope.** 3-month MVP with 8–10 characters worth of boxes; 6-month version
  with the fusion + shelf meta.
- **Closest existing.** *Vampire Survivors* meets *Loot Box Simulator*; the
  ritual half is genuinely uncontested.
- **Fatal flaw.** Gacha vibes are radioactive to a chunk of the audience.
  Needs zero real-money monetization and needs to communicate that in the
  first screenshot, or it gets pre-review-bombed.

### 2. Italian Brainrot: Wave Zero

**Pitch.** A wave shooter where every enemy is an AI-generated
animal/object hybrid — Bombardiro Crocodilo, Tralalero Tralala, Tung Tung Tung
Sahur — and the whole game speaks the meme's absurd Italian narration.

- **Core mechanic.** Standard bullet-heaven, but each enemy family has a
  chanted name and a two-line "lore" that pops when it first spawns; kill
  streaks trigger stacked audio layers of the brainrot chant. Weapons are
  themselves hybrids ("shark-espresso-machine" fires beans that grow legs).
  The joke is the density.
- **Why now?** The Italian-brainrot format is at peak virality this month and
  is *made for short clips*. A single 20-second gameplay reel of a
  crocodile-plane getting bonked reads instantly on TikTok, which is the only
  marketing lever a solo dev can pull.
- **Solo dev feasibility.** **7/10.** The mechanic is trivial; the risk is
  content volume and IP-adjacency. Do NOT use the actual meme names — invent
  parallel hybrids so you're riding the *format*, not scraping the memes.
- **Scope.** 3-month MVP with ~15 hybrids and one biome; 6-month if you want
  full VO and 3 biomes.
- **Closest existing.** *Vampire Survivors* + *Goat Simulator* tone; nothing
  is credibly targeting this exact audio-meme layer.
- **Fatal flaw.** Meme half-life is brutal. If you slip past ~Q1 2027, the
  entire aesthetic reads as embarrassing. Ship in 90 days or don't ship.

### 3. Yacht Club: A Quiet Massacre

**Pitch.** A wave shooter drenched in 90s old-money minimalism — you're a
socialite on a marble yacht deck, the "hordes" are paparazzi, fans, tabloid
drones, and every kill is scored to a single upright piano.

- **Core mechanic.** Slow, deliberate wave shooter (think one-shot revolver +
  cane parry, not machine-gun spray). Enemies drop *rumors* instead of XP;
  spend rumors between waves at the bar to buy passive social-power upgrades.
  Death is scandal, not blood — screen fades to a tabloid cover.
- **Why now?** The 90s Bessette-Kennedy / "old money" aesthetic is having a
  full-throated moment on TikTok as counter-programming to maximalist
  brainrot. Nobody has cashed it in on the game side. It also gives a solo
  dev a very cheap art direction — restrained palette, negative space, one
  environment.
- **Solo dev feasibility.** **9/10.** The aesthetic *is* the differentiator.
  Low enemy variety, low VFX budget, low map count all fit the fantasy. A
  single beautiful yacht scene can carry the whole game.
- **Scope.** Solid 4-month MVP; 6 months with a second locale (villa, gala).
- **Closest existing.** *Vampire Survivors* mechanics × *Hitman*'s tonal
  restraint; the fantasy is genuinely new to the genre.
- **Fatal flaw.** Slow-paced survivors games historically underperform. The
  "elegance" pitch has to survive contact with the dopamine loop — if
  playtests say "boring," restraint becomes the flaw.

## GAP IN MARKET (nobody has done this well yet)

### 4. Sidescroll Survivors

**Pitch.** A 2D side-scrolling wave survivor — you're always running right
through an endless corridor while the horde catches up behind you.

- **Core mechanic.** Auto-attack survivors upgrades, but the arena is a
  1D-with-jumps corridor: momentum matters, terrain interacts with weapons
  (fire on oil, ricochet off ceilings), and "waves" become density spikes
  ahead + a rising pressure wall behind you. Verticality is the new
  positioning skill.
- **Why now?** Multiple 2026 press pieces called the 2D side-scroller lane
  "woefully underserved" this year. Every Survivors-like is top-down; the
  side view is a free axis of differentiation, and it's a form every player
  parses in one screenshot.
- **Solo dev feasibility.** **9/10.** 2D physics is well-trodden Unity
  territory, side-scroll level pieces are cheap to generate procedurally, and
  camera work is simple.
- **Scope.** 3-month MVP absolutely doable; 6 months for meta-progression and
  boss encounters.
- **Closest existing.** Closest is *Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor* (top-down) or
  *Metal Slug* (not survivor). The exact intersection has no dominant title.
- **Fatal flaw.** Side-scroll can't do the "surrounded by 400 sprites" money
  shot that sells Survivors games in trailers. Have to invent a different
  hero moment — probably a "wall of hell chasing you" reveal.

### 5. Two-Stick Sofa

**Pitch.** Local + online 2-player couch co-op wave survivor with asymmetric
roles: one player is the *shooter*, the other is the *builder* placing walls,
turrets, and traps in real time.

- **Core mechanic.** P1 plays a normal survivors shooter; P2 has a floating
  cursor and a build tray, spending shared resources to shape the arena. You
  lose if either dies. Perks upgrade the *pair*, so builds have to be
  negotiated. Split-keyboard, 2-controller, or online-with-one-invite.
- **Why now?** Couch/co-op is a screaming market gap — the only recent hit
  serving it is PEAK, and it's not in this shape. Every big Survivors game is
  aggressively single-player. Streamers reliably lean into 2-player games
  because the second seat is content by itself.
- **Solo dev feasibility.** **6/10.** Network code is the tax. Ship
  local-only-first (couch or shared-screen), add online after launch. Unity's
  Netcode for GameObjects is workable but eats weeks.
- **Scope.** 4-month MVP couch-only; 8–10 months with online.
- **Closest existing.** *Death Must Die*'s co-op is closest, but roles are
  symmetric. Asymmetric shooter+builder in this genre is empty.
- **Fatal flaw.** Asymmetric co-op has a well-known "P2 gets bored / P2 is a
  worse role" trap. Balance is the entire design job, and it's the hardest
  kind to solo-playtest.

### 6. Mirror Match

**Pitch.** A wave survivor where every upgrade you pick, the enemy horde also
gets — so buildcrafting becomes a live conversation with the swarm.

- **Core mechanic.** Standard perk pick at level-up, but a visible timer
  counts down until enemies inherit your last choice (fire trail? now they
  leave fire trails). You can hard-counter your own build by picking
  something you know will hurt the incoming version of you. Late runs become
  a rock-paper-scissors dance against your past self.
- **Why now?** Survivors-genre discourse this year is fixated on the "runs
  end because you're too strong, not because it's hard" problem. Mirror-match
  fixes it structurally without adding difficulty sliders. It also generates
  outrageous clip moments ("I gave them my whole build and it killed me").
- **Solo dev feasibility.** **8/10.** No new art or systems — it's a rule
  layer on top of the existing upgrade pool. The design work is picking which
  perks are legal to mirror.
- **Scope.** 3-month MVP; the whole idea is a twist, not a content pile.
- **Closest existing.** Nothing exact. Bloodstained's chaos mode and Isaac's
  T. Lost gesture at it. Untapped in Survivors-likes.
- **Fatal flaw.** Skill floor gets steep fast. A new player who doesn't
  understand the mirror rule feels punished for progressing, which is the
  worst-possible new-player experience in this genre.

## WILDCARD / EXPERIMENTAL

### 7. Horde Boss

**Pitch.** A wave survivor from the enemy's POV — you're the necromancer
watching a lone hero mow through your mobs, and your job is to spend souls
between waves to buy the composition, positioning, and elite modifiers that
finally end them.

- **Core mechanic.** No twin-stick shooting. Between waves, you drag units
  onto lanes, pick modifiers ("this batch dodges the aura weapon"), and
  watch. The hero is scripted AI running a Vampire-Survivors-style build; you
  are the roguelike deckbuilder trying to counter it. Win = they die before
  minute 30.
- **Why now?** "Reverse-genre" hits (Plague Inc, We Are The Villains,
  auto-battler renaissance) prove the appetite. And the Survivors format is
  well enough understood by the audience now that you can *invert* it and
  people get the joke immediately.
- **Solo dev feasibility.** **7/10.** No shooting means no gunfeel to nail,
  but the hero-AI is now the hardest system in the game and it needs to feel
  legit alive.
- **Scope.** 6 months to MVP honestly; the AI + counter-vocabulary needs
  content depth or it's shallow after one run.
- **Closest existing.** *Reus*, *Cursed Crew*, kind of *Slice & Dice*. No
  direct competitor in the Survivors-inverse space.
- **Fatal flaw.** "Watching" games are hard to sell — the store page has to
  make the strategic-input half look active, or it reads as an idle game and
  gets priced accordingly.

### 8. Six Seven

**Pitch.** A rhythm wave-shooter that runs on user-supplied music: enemies
spawn on the beat, your gun only fires on the beat, and every run you drop
in a track and get a procedurally-arranged level built around its waveform.

- **Core mechanic.** Standard top-down shooter with a beat-locked trigger.
  Dodges cost a half-beat of stamina; picking up "the drop" fills a
  screen-clearing super. Bring your own MP3 / Spotify link / SoundCloud
  track; the game analyzes tempo + energy and builds the enemy timeline live.
  Global leaderboards per song.
- **Why now?** The "Six Seven" audio meme, per-song TikTok challenges, and
  the resurgence of *Beat Saber*-adjacent format all point at the same
  cultural nerve: people want games shaped around music they already care
  about. Nothing in the wave-survival lane touches this.
- **Solo dev feasibility.** **6/10.** Real-time audio analysis in Unity is
  doable (FFT + onset detection) but eats a month, and licensing is a
  quicksand pit — the answer is user-supplied files only, never bundled
  music.
- **Scope.** 6-month MVP; audio DSP is where all the schedule risk lives.
- **Closest existing.** *Audiosurf*, *BPM: Bullets Per Minute*. Both are
  cult-tier and old enough that a modern take with user music is a real
  opening.
- **Fatal flaw.** Music copyright politics on streaming (DMCA'd VODs) can
  strangle the streamer marketing loop that a solo dev needs to survive.
  Ship with a permissive royalty-free starter pack so streamers have safe
  material.

## Recommendation for this dev

If forced to pick one from an existing wave-shooter codebase:

1. **Mirror Match (#6)** — highest hit rate per hour of work, lowest new-art
   cost, best chance the current codebase carries most of it.
2. **Sidescroll Survivors (#4)** — cleanest "screenshot tells the whole game"
   pitch, largest empty market lane.
3. **Blind Box Survivor (#1)** — best trend timing and streamable ritual, but
   burn the runway carefully — 90-day window before the culture moves.

Concepts to *avoid unless you love them*: #2 (meme half-life) and #5
(networking eats a solo dev alive).
