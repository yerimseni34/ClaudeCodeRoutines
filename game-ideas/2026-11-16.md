# Game Concept Brainstorm — 2026-11-16

Target developer: solo dev, 1–2 years Unity experience, currently building a wave survival shooter. Concepts below bias toward adjacent mechanics (wave defense, horde survival, shooter loops) so the current project's tooling and knowledge carry over.

## Market context (what fed these ideas)

- **Bullet Heaven is officially a Steam tag (May 2026).** Megabonk (3D Vampire Survivors-like) crossed a million copies in two weeks; Hordes of Hunger, Warhammer Survivors, Horde Reapers all arrived in 2026. The genre is crowded but still minting breakouts when an entry adds a real new dimension.
- **Indie non-sequel breakouts spiked in 2026.** Sensor Tower data (via PC Gamer) credits indies with 20 of 21 extra breakout launches vs. the prior cohort. Escape From Duckov (friendslop extraction shooter), Cairn (mountaineering survival, ~300k in week one), and Megabonk are the standout debut names.
- **Cultural moments in the air:** the 2025 Louvre heist still has meme legs, "Italian Brainrot" / AI-slop aesthetic has graduated from Skibidi Toilet to a durable visual language (TikTok, Nov 2026), Stranger Things wrapped Dec 2025 and its 80s nostalgia halo is still huge, Winter Olympics 2026 hype, Bieberchella, Conformity Gate, Labubu holdover.
- **Solo-dev scope reality check:** blogs converge on 3–6 months for a tightly-scoped 2D single-mechanic game, 6–12 months for something with real depth, and multiplayer reliably triples timelines. Scores below assume 2D or stylized-3D, no netcode, mostly asset-store/kitbash art.

## 1. Italian Brainrot Siege  _(trend)_

1. **Pitch:** A wave shooter where the enemies are AI-slop creatures — Tralalero Tralala sharks in Nikes, Ballerina Cappuccina, bombardino crocodilos — and every death plays a nonsense voiceover.
2. **Core mechanic:** Classic top-down horde shooter loop. The twist: enemies have absurd physics (the shark hops, the ballerina pirouettes, the crocodile divebombs) that force you to read silhouettes under maximum visual chaos.
3. **Why now?** Italian Brainrot is still trending on TikTok in Nov 2026 per SocialPilot's roundup; the aesthetic is recognizable to Gen Z/Alpha and is marketing fuel on its own. No one has shipped a polished shooter wrapped in it yet — just itch.io jam entries.
4. **Feasibility: 7/10.** AI-generated voice lines and simple 2D/low-poly art play into the aesthetic; "ugly on purpose" is a license to ship fast.
5. **Scope: 3–4 months** for a 10-minute roguelite loop; 6 months with a meta-progression layer.
6. **Closest existing:** Brotato, Megabonk, Vampire Survivors — mechanically. Aesthetically, nothing shipped yet.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Brainrot has a short half-life. If it dies before launch the whole hook evaporates, and the game underneath has to stand on generic horde-shooter mechanics against a genre full of polished competitors.

## 2. Louvre: Last Stand  _(trend)_

1. **Pitch:** You pulled the heist. Now hold the vault for seven minutes until the getaway driver arrives, while Paris police rappel through skylights.
2. **Core mechanic:** Wave defense in a single tight room. Between waves you reinforce doors, rig the Mona Lisa as a shield, and choose which painting to carry out (heavier = better loot score but slower exit).
3. **Why now?** The October 2025 Louvre heist is the year's most memeable real event and still lives in Good Morning America retrospectives — recognizable premise with zero IP cost.
4. **Feasibility: 8/10.** One room, one objective, no procedural generation needed. Fits the dev's wave-shooter codebase almost directly.
5. **Scope: 3 months** for a vertical slice with 3 heist locations; 6 months for a full roster of museums/vaults and meta progression.
6. **Closest existing:** PAYDAY 3's holdout phases, Zombie Panic in Wonderland, the "Loud & Clear" mission in Lethal Company.
7. **Fatal flaw:** A single-room premise fatigues fast. Needs strong procedural wrinkles (random art layout, random guard patterns) or it's a 30-minute game.

## 3. Alpine Hold  _(trend)_

1. **Pitch:** Cairn's mountaineering, but at night the mountain sends things down at your base camp — and you shoot back.
2. **Core mechanic:** Day phase: climb a procedural peak, place anchors, scavenge. Night phase: tower-defense-with-a-rifle at your tent, using the anchors you placed as choke points.
3. **Why now?** Cairn was one of the year's clearest indie wins (Comicbook.com's op-ed, 300k players in week one), and its calm-mountaineering vibe hasn't been crossed with combat yet. A dark-fantasy spin lets you ride the wave without being derivative.
4. **Feasibility: 6/10.** The climb system is real work — rope physics, grip stamina — but you can crib from Jusant's design. Wave combat you already have.
5. **Scope: 8–12 months.** Two tightly-coupled systems both need polish; this is the longest build on the list.
6. **Closest existing:** Jusant (climbing), They Are Billions (base defense), Valheim (day/night loop). No one hybrid.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Two complete gameplay systems in one game = double the places to fail. If climbing isn't satisfying on its own, nothing else saves it.

## 4. Bullet Heaven FPS  _(gap)_

1. **Pitch:** Vampire Survivors in first person. You walk forward, your guns fire themselves, and the horde closes in from every direction.
2. **Core mechanic:** FPS movement + auto-firing weapons mounted on the player (shoulder cannons, orbital drones, floor-stomp AoE). You pick loadout each run; positioning and strafe timing replace the top-down "find the gap" dance.
3. **Why now?** Megabonk proved 3D bullet heaven works and sold a million. Nobody has shipped a first-person entry yet, and FPS is the most-played PC perspective. Clean genre whitespace with proven demand on either side.
4. **Feasibility: 7/10.** First-person controller is a solved problem in Unity; the hard part is readability of a 360° swarm from eye level.
5. **Scope: 6 months** for a solid loop with 8–10 weapon archetypes; 9 months with meta progression.
6. **Closest existing:** Megabonk (3rd person 3D), Risk of Rain Returns (2.5D), Gunfire Reborn (FPS roguelite, but you aim). None are hands-off first-person.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Swarm readability at eye level. If players can't see what's about to hit them, the game feels unfair instead of overwhelming — and that gap closes within ~6 months of being proven viable.

## 5. Diegetic Horde Shooter  _(gap)_

1. **Pitch:** A wave shooter with zero HUD. Ammo, health, cooldowns, map, wave number — all physically rendered on your weapon.
2. **Core mechanic:** Standard horde shooter, but you read your state by glancing at the gun: iron sight shows a heartbeat gauge, a side panel shows ammo, a flipped grip shows the minimap. Weapons are the UI.
3. **Why now?** Metroid Prime's visor philosophy has fans but almost no horde-shooter inheritor. Streamers love "clean" footage with no HUD overlay, which is free marketing. Accessibility-conscious design is also having a moment.
4. **Feasibility: 8/10.** The system is mostly art + shader work on the weapon model. No new backend.
5. **Scope: 4–5 months** for a focused arena-mode release; 7 months with a short campaign.
6. **Closest existing:** Dead Space (diegetic health bar), Metroid Prime (visor UI), Receiver 2 (diegetic weapon handling). None are horde-shooters.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Readability under pressure. If players can't parse their state at a glance during a 200-enemy wave, they'll bounce in 20 minutes. Needs heavy playtest iteration, which is painful solo.

## 6. Ghost-Run Co-op  _(gap)_

1. **Pitch:** A solo wave shooter where your friends' past runs leave ghosts, supply caches, and sometimes zombified versions of themselves on the map.
2. **Core mechanic:** Single-player horde survival. Friend-code or Steam-friends tether; each run uploads a replay trace. On your next run their ghost appears as a time-delayed ally that shoots at what they shot at, and their dropped loot persists until a wave consumes it.
3. **Why now?** Death Stranding 2 (2025) rebooted async multiplayer in the mainstream conversation; Lethal Company and REPO proved social-but-small co-op appetite. No horde shooter has tried the Death Stranding social model yet.
4. **Feasibility: 5/10.** Async networking is less scary than realtime but still a networking project. The dev's stated inexperience with multiplayer is the biggest flag.
5. **Scope: 9–12 months.** Could be cut to 6 by shipping solo-only first and adding ghosts in an update.
6. **Closest existing:** Death Stranding (async traces), Elden Ring (messages/ghosts), Loop Hero (passive ally traces). No horde-shooter hybrid.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Async co-op lives or dies on player base. Below ~10k DAU the ghost density is too sparse to feel social and the mechanic dies — chicken-and-egg for a solo launch.

## 7. Granny's Garden Defense  _(wildcard)_

1. **Pitch:** A cozy wave shooter: defend grandma's prize tulips from pests that start as slugs and end as Lovecraftian root-gods.
2. **Core mechanic:** Isometric defense. Weapons are garden tools: water can (pistol), hose (shotgun), weed-whacker (chainsaw), sprinkler (turret). Between waves you plant flowers that double as currency and traps.
3. **Why now?** Cozy-game audience keeps growing (Cozy Game Reviews, Palia, Fields of Mistria); horror-cozy tonal mashups (Cult of the Lamb, Spiritfarer) consistently overperform. No cozy wave shooter exists.
4. **Feasibility: 9/10.** 2D isometric art is cheap, mechanics are a direct skin on the dev's existing wave shooter, "cute but weird" art style forgives rough edges.
5. **Scope: 3–4 months** for a complete loop with 20 waves and a boss; 6 months with meta progression and seasons.
6. **Closest existing:** Plants vs. Zombies (closest tonally and mechanically), Cult of the Lamb (horror-cozy tone), Brotato (pacing).
7. **Fatal flaw:** PvZ owns this mental space so thoroughly that any comparison kills the pitch. Needs one standout mechanic (the shifting-to-horror arc might be it) or it reads as "PvZ but worse."

## 8. One-Thumb Horde  _(wildcard)_

1. **Pitch:** A wave survival shooter with one input. Hold to aim-and-charge, release to fire. That's the whole control scheme.
2. **Core mechanic:** Auto-walking character; player's only input controls a radial aim that sweeps around them while held, then fires a shaped shot on release (longer hold = wider/stronger arc). Movement handled by the game.
3. **Why now?** Accessibility is a louder design conversation every year; TikTok-native vertical phone play rewards one-thumb games; Downwell-style radical simplification periodically breaks through (Luck Be a Landlord, Balatro). Zero-input-count horde shooter is genuinely novel.
4. **Feasibility: 10/10.** Input is literally one bool. Perfect portfolio piece.
5. **Scope: 2–3 months** for a complete game; 4 months with full polish and a mobile port.
6. **Closest existing:** Downwell (minimal input), One Finger Death Punch, Holedown. None are wave shooters.
7. **Fatal flaw:** The constraint can feel like a gimmick 10 minutes in. Needs enough weapon variety and enemy types that the single input keeps producing new decisions — otherwise it's a demo, not a game.

---

## Shortlist recommendation

If forced to rank by expected shipped-and-noticed probability for this specific dev:

1. **Granny's Garden Defense** — highest feasibility, strongest tonal hook, closest fit to the current codebase.
2. **Louvre: Last Stand** — tight scope, strong premise, almost drops into the existing project.
3. **Bullet Heaven FPS** — biggest commercial upside; genuine market gap; moderate risk on readability.

Avoid Ghost-Run Co-op unless the dev wants a networking learning project rather than a shipping project.
