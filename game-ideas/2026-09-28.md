# Game Concept Ideation — 2026-09-28

**For:** Solo indie dev, 1-2 yrs Unity XP, wave-survival-shooter foundation
**Method:** Trend scan across Steam, TikTok/Reddit cultural pulse, and gap analysis of the bullet-heaven / horde-shooter landscape.

## Market context (why these picks)

- The bullet-heaven / Vampire-Survivors-like genre is officially a Steam tag as of May 2026 — mature, but saturated with reskins. Differentiation, not template chasing, is what sells.
- The *Backrooms* film (Kane Parsons, A24) hit theaters Sep 25, 2026, reigniting liminal-space horror at box-office scale. ~600 Backrooms games already on Steam, but almost none combine liminal dread with wave-shooter cadence.
- Jimothy the Raccoon (Seattle, viral Sept 2026) is a shared cultural in-joke. The "unbothered blorbo" energy maps well to charming solo-dev-scale characters.
- Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor (Unity, 2025) proved auto-shooter + strong IP hook = commercial hit. The template-plus-a-twist is a proven solo path.
- Player preference in 2026 leans toward *systemic depth* over reflex — build variety, resource management, and tactical decisions beat twitch aim.

## Trend-based concepts (riding the current wave)

### 1. Liminal Waves *(Backrooms + bullet heaven)*
1. **Pitch:** A reverse-bullet-hell where every wave rearranges a Backrooms level around you and enemies are the *sounds* leaking through the walls.
2. **Core mechanic:** Auto-attack against hordes that spawn from vents, corners, and reflections; between waves, you *walk* through procedurally shuffled liminal rooms picking the next hallway (each hallway = a build modifier: fluorescent-flicker crit, damp-carpet slow aura, etc.).
3. **Why now?** Backrooms film opens weekend of Sep 25, 2026 — massive discovery tailwind. No shipped Backrooms game currently uses the bullet-heaven loop; the intersection is empty.
4. **Solo dev feasibility:** 8/10. Aesthetic is *cheap by design* (fluorescent lights, empty rooms = fewer assets). Wave-shooter code you already have transfers directly.
5. **Scope:** 6 months to a strong demo/EA; 9-12 months for 1.0.
6. **Closest existing:** *Escape the Backrooms* (co-op walking horror, no combat loop) + *Brotato* (arena horde). Nothing sits in the middle.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Trademark/IP anxiety around "Backrooms" branding post-A24 film. Ship as "liminal horror" and let the aesthetic do the marketing.

### 2. Trash Panda: A Jimothy Story
1. **Pitch:** You are an unbothered raccoon defending your dumpster from waves of animal control, HOA drones, and rival wildlife.
2. **Core mechanic:** Wave shooter where projectiles are *items you fling from your trash pile* — banana peels (slip), pizza slices (heal), fireworks (AOE). Between waves you dumpster-dive to restock; the trash you eat also mutates the raccoon (bigger, glowier, weirder builds).
3. **Why now?** Jimothy the Raccoon dominated Sept 2026 TikTok; "unbothered animal" is a shared cultural mood right now. Charming, meme-adjacent IP that spreads on socials for free.
4. **Solo dev feasibility:** 9/10. Cartoon low-poly art scales, jokes carry weak visuals, gif-able moments are marketing gold.
5. **Scope:** 3-4 months to a shippable roguelite. Ideal starter project.
6. **Closest existing:** *Brotato* (potato + guns) — you're doing the same trick with a hotter mascot.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Meme half-life. Jimothy is a Sept 2026 moment; if you take 2 years the joke is dead. Ship fast or reframe as generic "urban wildlife" if the meme cools.

### 3. Slow Horses: London Standoff
1. **Pitch:** A wave-defense shooter cast as a burned-out MI5 agent defending Slough House from wave after wave of assets, moles, and Park suits.
2. **Core mechanic:** Cover-based auto-fire; between waves you draft *dossier cards* (informants, leaked files, favors owed) instead of weapons. Some cards betray you — reveal a mole mid-wave and enemies spawn *inside* your cover.
3. **Why now?** *Slow Horses* S6 hits Apple TV Sept 16, 2026 — audience is peaking and there is no tie-in game or unofficial homage in the espionage-defense niche.
4. **Solo dev feasibility:** 6/10. Requires personality and writing muscle; art can be pixel/comic-panel style to stay solo-scoped.
5. **Scope:** 9-12 months. Writing is the long pole, not code.
6. **Closest existing:** *Cultist Simulator* (tone) + *Luck Be A Landlord* (card horde defense). No spy-horde-defense hybrid I can find.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Espionage-genre fans want narrative depth solo devs struggle to deliver. If writing is not your strength this collapses into a reskin.

## Gap-in-market concepts (nobody made these well yet)

### 4. Wave-Shooter with a *Real* Economy
1. **Pitch:** A horde shooter where the enemies also *shop* — a shared marketplace where you and the horde bid on upgrades between waves.
2. **Core mechanic:** Standard auto-fire during waves; between waves both sides visit the same shop. Enemies get smarter/stronger from what *you don't buy*. Every purchase is also a denial.
3. **Why now?** 2026 players openly want "strategy, resource management, tactical decisions" (see the shooter-innovation reporting). No bullet-heaven treats the shop as a two-sided auction — it's an obvious empty seat.
4. **Solo dev feasibility:** 7/10. Simple visuals fine; the risk is *systems tuning* — the AI-buying loop must feel fair.
5. **Scope:** 6 months minimum; the balancing tail is the real cost.
6. **Closest existing:** *Backpack Battles* (auto-battler shop tension) + *Vampire Survivors* (horde). No one has fused them at the horde-shooter altitude.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Solo devs historically underestimate economy tuning; if unbalanced, one shop item breaks every run and the whole gimmick with it.

### 5. Auto-Shooter That Uses *Your Body*
1. **Pitch:** A wave-survival shooter where the level layout, spawn cadence, and difficulty adapt to your posture via webcam pose detection — lean in to charge, lean out to dodge, hands up to reload.
2. **Core mechanic:** MediaPipe/similar pose input feeds a stress meter; the game reads your body language and escalates when you're comfortable, backs off when it sees you tense.
3. **Why now?** Cheap on-device pose models (MediaPipe, Unity Sentis) matured in 2025-2026; nobody's shipped an auto-shooter that uses them. Streaming-friendly (webcam is already on).
4. **Solo dev feasibility:** 5/10. Pose input is fiddly; accessibility fallback (keyboard) mandatory.
5. **Scope:** 9-12 months, mostly UX and calibration.
6. **Closest existing:** *Nevermind* (biofeedback horror) — but no one has done this in an auto-shooter.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Accessibility. Any body-input game loses disabled players unless a full non-body mode exists — which then risks feeling like a gimmick tacked onto a normal shooter.

### 6. Wave Shooter × Post-Mortem Detective
1. **Pitch:** You survive a wave, die, and the *next* run you play a detective reconstructing what killed the previous you.
2. **Core mechanic:** Alternating runs: (A) a normal wave-shooter run that logs every input/enemy spawn; (B) a slow deductive run where clues (bullet holes, dropped items, spawn patterns) explain how run A ended, and solving them buys permanent upgrades.
3. **Why now?** Reverse-detective indie hits (a Unity-solo-dev reverse-detective adventure took off in 2026) proved appetite for inverted mystery loops. No auto-shooter has borrowed the trick.
4. **Solo dev feasibility:** 7/10. Reuses your existing wave code twice — once live, once as a replay/inspection view.
5. **Scope:** 6-9 months.
6. **Closest existing:** *Return of the Obra Dinn* (post-mortem deduction) + any survivor-like. No hybrid exists.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Two loops = two things to balance. If the detective loop is weaker than the action loop, players will just skip it.

## Wildcard / experimental concepts

### 7. Handcuff SpongeBob: The Escape Shooter
1. **Pitch:** A wave shooter where your gun is *stuck to your wrists* and every upgrade makes it slide off easier — creating a physics-comedy tension between firepower and clumsiness.
2. **Core mechanic:** Physics-driven weapon attachment. Upgrades add weight, size, or slipperiness; drop your weapon mid-wave and you have to grab it back with the horde on you. Riffs on the SpongeBob handcuffs meme (2017, re-viral Sept 2026).
3. **Why now?** Physics comedy (Human Fall Flat, Only Up) still moves streams. Meme fuel is free marketing.
4. **Solo dev feasibility:** 6/10. Physics tuning is unforgiving; a jank moment sinks the joke.
5. **Scope:** 6 months, plus streamer beta for tuning "watchable jank."
6. **Closest existing:** *Gang Beasts* physics + any horde shooter. No overlap.
7. **Fatal flaw:** "Funny physics" that isn't funny is just broken controls. Unity's physics can betray a solo dev — one bad update and the joke dies.

### 8. American Girl Doll: Wave Defense
1. **Pitch:** A dollhouse-scale tower/wave defense where your protagonists are 90s-era toy-line dolls with unhinged backstories, screaming "MIA!" every crit.
2. **Core mechanic:** Tiny-scale tabletop wave defense; dolls have "accessory slots" (backpack, hairbrush, tea set) that stack into unhinged combos. Voice-acted crit calls lifted from the "my American girl doll" audio trend.
3. **Why now?** The "my American girl doll Mia" TikTok sound is peaking Sept 2026 and skews female-audience — a demographic bullet-heaven under-serves.
4. **Solo dev feasibility:** 8/10. Small dioramas = small asset lists. Voice work is the biggest ask; a friend with a mic solves it.
5. **Scope:** 4-6 months.
6. **Closest existing:** *Kingdom Rush* (tower defense charm) + *Cult of the Lamb* (voice character). No doll-scale horde-defense entry exists.
7. **Fatal flaw:** Licensing minefield if it drifts too close to real doll brands. Keep it obviously parody and generic.
