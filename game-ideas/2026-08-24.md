## Trend-based (riding a current wave)

### 1. Level Zero
- **Pitch:** A one-stick wave shooter set entirely inside the Backrooms — every "level" is a different liminal biome (poolrooms, mall food court, empty office), and the enemies are the wallpaper.
- **Core mechanic:** Fixed-arena wave defense where the *arena itself* mutates each round (fluorescent tubes flicker out, hallways extend, doors reroute). You fight to keep the exit door within line-of-sight.
- **Why now?** The A24 Backrooms feature (Kane Parsons) put liminal horror back on the front page, and #liminalspaces is at 2B+ TikTok views. Cozy horror and paranormal thrillers are the fastest-growing dark-fiction subgenres of 2026. This is the visual moment.
- **Solo dev feasibility:** 8/10. Liminal aesthetic is *forgiving* — flat textures, low geo, blown-out lighting. Enemies can be silhouettes/entities, no need for character rigs.
- **Scope:** 3-month vertical slice (one biome, five entities); 6-month full release (5 biomes, meta run structure).
- **Closest existing game:** *Escape the Backrooms* (co-op exploration) — no one has nailed a *shooter* framing yet.
- **Fatal flaw:** Backrooms fandom is genre-loyal to walking-sim horror; a shooter framing may read as "missing the point" and get review-bombed by purists. Framing/marketing must lean *survival horror*, not action.

### 2. Slop Purge
- **Pitch:** A wave shooter where the enemies are AI-generated abominations spilling out of a corrupted image-gen server — six-fingered hands, warped mascot brands, TTS-babbling politicians.
- **Core mechanic:** Standard survivor auto-fire, but every enemy has a "prompt tag" cloud around it. You gain temporary buffs by shooting enemies whose tags contradict each other (`cozy` + `feral`). Between waves you "unplug" GPUs to lower the spawn ceiling.
- **Why now?** 2026 is the year of the AI backlash — Commonwealth Short Story Prize controversy, MIT's "95% of GenAI pilots fail" number in circulation, and popular anger at "AI necklaces" and slop feeds. A game that lets players *shoot the slop* has a built-in cultural cheer.
- **Solo dev feasibility:** 7/10. Enemy variety is the risk — the joke needs 30+ distinct silhouettes to keep landing. Procedural mashup sprites (two random enemy halves stitched) could cheat this well.
- **Scope:** 6 months. The bit needs polish or it dies as a one-note tweet.
- **Closest existing game:** *Nothing direct.* *Hi-Fi Rush* and *High on Life* are the closest in "shooter with a strong voice."
- **Fatal flaw:** The topic ages fast. If AI hype cycle turns again in 12 months this reads as dated. Also risks looking like the very slop it mocks unless art direction is *deliberately* hand-crafted and signed.

### 3. Reset '16
- **Pitch:** A wave shooter drowning in 2016 nostalgia — you defend a Pokémon-GO-style park meetup from waves of vine-star zombies, Snapchat-filter demons, and the ghost of Harambe.
- **Core mechanic:** Standard wave defense, but weapons and upgrades are 2016 memes: dab-cannon (crowd stagger), damn-daniel boots (dash), mannequin-challenge grenade (freezes enemies mid-animation). Between-round meta uses a fake Vine-style 6-second replay of your best kill.
- **Why now?** "2026 is the new 2016" is the meme thread of the year, and the nostalgia cycle has clearly closed the decade. TikTok users are actively reposting 2016 aesthetics, music, filters. This lands on the wave, not behind it.
- **Solo dev feasibility:** 9/10. Almost every asset is a joke; pixel/lo-fi 2016-web art is cheap and *thematically correct*. The hardest part is licensing — solo dev should use *evocative* parody, not actual IP.
- **Scope:** 3 months to a demo, 6 months to full release. This is the concept most likely to actually ship.
- **Closest existing game:** *Frog Fractions*, *Enter the Gungeon* in tone. No wave shooter has claimed this vein.
- **Fatal flaw:** The nostalgia audience is thin outside 25–35yr-olds who lived it, and the meme cycle may crash by 2027. Ship fast or don't ship.

## Gap-in-market (nobody has done this well yet)

### 4. Shutter
- **Pitch:** A wave "shooter" where your only weapon is a camera — the better the framing, focus, and composition of your shot, the more damage it deals.
- **Core mechanic:** Enemies approach; you snap photos. A composition grader (rule of thirds, focus lock, negative space, timing) awards damage and a photo score. Great shots become mounted trophies in your gallery that give passive perks next run.
- **Why now?** Indie exploration was the #1-search January 2026 indie niche, and "photography games" (*Umurangi*, *Toem*, *Pupperazzi*) are a proven-but-tiny genre. Nobody has crossed photography with survivor combat. Rewards deliberate play over twitch.
- **Solo dev feasibility:** 6/10. Camera framing math is achievable in Unity (RenderTexture + heuristic scoring); the *hard* problem is making every enemy interesting to *look at*, not just to shoot.
- **Scope:** 6–9 months. Composition grading needs iteration.
- **Closest existing game:** *Umurangi Generation* (no combat), *Pokémon Snap* (rails). The combat + composition intersection is empty.
- **Fatal flaw:** Composition grading is subjective — if the algorithm feels arbitrary, players revolt within an hour. Needs a *very* transparent scoring UI.

### 5. Kettle & Coven
- **Pitch:** A cozy wave-defense game where a witch defends her cottage from restless spirits, and every wave-break is a real tea-brewing minigame that determines your next round's buffs.
- **Core mechanic:** Waves are short (60–90s) and readable — a small circle of familiars auto-fires charms. Between waves is a tactile kitchen: pick herbs, temp your water, choose steep time. Brew quality gates upgrades.
- **Why now?** Cozy paranormal and supernatural cozy-horror are two of the fastest-growing crossover niches per 2026 dark-fiction industry reporting. Cozy games (*Stardew*, *Cozy Grove*) are proven; nobody has fused cozy with *combat-loop survival*.
- **Solo dev feasibility:** 9/10. Small arena, short waves, low enemy count, forgiving art direction. Tea minigame is a self-contained UI screen. This is the shortest path to a *finished* game.
- **Scope:** 3–4 months. Genuinely doable as an "August-to-Christmas" release.
- **Closest existing game:** *Coffee Talk* (no combat) + *Vampire Survivors* (no cozy). Overlap is empty.
- **Fatal flaw:** Cozy audiences are conflict-averse. If death feels punitive the tag chases them off; if it doesn't, survivor fans lose interest. The tea minigame *must* carry the "cozy" tag alone.

### 6. Chatter
- **Pitch:** A 2–4 player co-op wave shooter where you can't see each other and can only communicate via typed messages that appear as floating chat bubbles in the world.
- **Core mechanic:** Standard co-op wave defense with a critical constraint — no voice, no minimap, no player nameplates. Your only info about teammates is what they *type*. Enemies are drawn to chat bubbles. Silence = safety = coordination failure.
- **Why now?** "Weirdo indie co-op" is the emerging subgenre per 2026 indie market analysis (see *Chained Together*, *Content Warning*, *R.E.P.O.*). Communication-constraint co-op ("keep talking and nobody explodes", *Lethal Company*'s walkie range) is proven to go viral on Twitch/TikTok. No one has done it in a *shooter*.
- **Solo dev feasibility:** 6/10. Netcode for a solo dev is the risk (Photon Fusion or Netcode-for-Entities is achievable but a real learning tax). Everything else is cheap.
- **Scope:** 9–12 months, mostly because of netcode.
- **Closest existing game:** *Lethal Company* (walkie constraint) meets *Helldivers 2* (co-op shooter). Neither has the "chat draws enemies" hook.
- **Fatal flaw:** Multiplayer is a solo-dev death sentence if adoption is slow — empty lobbies kill the loop in a week. Needs bot fallback or a very small player-count target (2p).

## Wildcard / experimental

### 7. The Wave
- **Pitch:** You are the horde. Command 500 tiny agents to overwhelm a fortified survivor base run by procedurally-generated NPCs who play like actual survivor-game players.
- **Core mechanic:** RTS-lite swarm control (paint-path movement, three ability spends per wave), inverted from typical wave defense. Enemy NPCs auto-fire and level up between waves *just like a survivor game* — and the more they survive, the harder they become. You need to snuff them out before they hit a build power spike.
- **Why now?** The wave-shooter formula has been imitated for four years; the inversion is unclaimed. "Play the other side" games (*Dungeon Keeper*, *Evil Genius*) have a devoted but underserved audience. Perfect for a dev who already understands survivor AI from the inside.
- **Solo dev feasibility:** 5/10. Simulating enemy-NPCs-that-play-like-players is a deep AI problem. But the dev's existing wave shooter is *literally the AI's playbook* — huge reuse potential.
- **Scope:** 9–12 months. Longer if the NPC AI needs to feel intelligent.
- **Closest existing game:** *They Are Billions* (RTS defense) inverted. *Bad North*. Direct competitor is *thin*.
- **Fatal flaw:** Playing the "winning" side isn't as satisfying as the underdog — every kill can feel cheap. Needs strong per-agent identification (name your favourites, mourn them) or the swarm reads as spreadsheet.

### 8. Loop Feed
- **Pitch:** A wave survivor where between-round downtime is a Factorio-lite conveyor puzzle — your guns don't reload, they're fed by belts you route from ammo mines to turret placements.
- **Core mechanic:** 45-second waves alternate with 90-second build phases. Belts, splitters, and a limited grid. Guns starve if you route badly. Later waves force you to *rearrange* infrastructure under fire.
- **Why now?** Automation/sandbox is the fastest-growing indie subgenre (16.78% CAGR per 2026 market analysis), and StarRupture proved players want survival + factory. Nobody has cross-bred it with the survivor loop.
- **Solo dev feasibility:** 6/10. Belt simulation is a known Unity problem (well-documented ECS/DOTS pattern), but tuning the interplay between two very different game modes is where solo devs sink.
- **Scope:** 9 months.
- **Closest existing game:** *Mindustry* is the closest cousin (tower defense + factory). No survivor equivalent yet.
- **Fatal flaw:** Two audiences, neither fully served. Factorio fans want deeper automation; survivor fans want more juice. Risk: falls between two chairs.

## Recommendation snapshot

If I had to pick two to prototype this month, **Kettle & Coven** (#5) is the shortest-path-to-shipping and rides a demonstrably growing niche, and **Reset '16** (#3) is the most timely — the "2026 is the new 2016" wave is peaking *now* and won't wait for a year-long build. **Level Zero** (#1) is the highest ceiling but the highest risk of fandom friction. Everything else is a longer bet.
