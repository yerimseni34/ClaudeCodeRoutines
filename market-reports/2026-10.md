## Read this first: data quality

This report rests on web search results and a handful of fetched articles. Public data on small indie games is thin, so:

- **Hard numbers are scarce.** Most revenue figures below are third-party estimates (GameDiscoverCo, press reports). Sources sometimes disagree. Conflicts are flagged.
- **"Why it succeeded" is partly inference.** Where a source states a reason, it is marked *(sourced)*. Where it is my analysis, it is marked *(analysis)*.
- **Not every pick fits your filters.** Team size and price are missing for several games. Exceptions to "<10 people / <$20 / last 30 days" are called out.
- **Marketing details are mostly unknown.** Few sources document specific TikTok/Reddit/Discord campaigns. I say "not documented" rather than guess.

## 1. How to Fish — Dazed Games

| | |
|---|---|
| Genre / platform | Co-op physics fishing chaos game, Steam (PC) |
| Price | $7.99 list, launched at $4.95 (intro discount to Aug 27) |
| Team | 2 programmers (Carl-Vilhelm Johansson, Melvin Olsson), Sweden |
| Sales | Reports range widely: ~6.3M units / ~$26M gross in August (GDCo-sourced report); recap sites say 10M+ in under two months. The studio's "1M" figure was *players*, and some outlets misreported it as copies sold. |
| Peak CCU | ~341k–371k (Aug 25), sources differ |
| Reviews | ~94% positive, 16k+ reviews |

**Caveat:** Launched Aug 20, outside the 30-day window. It is included because it is still the dominant indie story and the clearest template of the year.

**Why it worked**
- Low price removes friction. At ~$5 a group of four friends can each buy it on impulse *(sourced)*.
- Fishing loop plus chaos escalation (gambling, trick shots, weapons, bosses) generates unscripted, clippable moments *(sourced)*.
- Fits the "friendslop" co-op wave that Lethal Company and PEAK established *(sourced)*.
- Launch pattern: modest day one, then a wall of co-streams, then peak concurrency about 14x launch-day three days later *(sourced)*.
- Patch 1.0.4 raised lobbies from 4 to 8 players, then added private sessions and stability fixes. Fast post-launch support kept momentum *(sourced)*.

**Marketing breakdown:** No campaign is documented. It is pure word of mouth and streamer pickup, helped by the intro discount. It looks more like product-market fit than a marketing plan. Whether the devs seeded creators is unknown.

**Solo-replicable**
- Design for "clip-ability": something absurd happens every 2–3 minutes.
- Price at $5–8 so a friend group buys as a unit.
- Build 4-player co-op from day one, and plan to scale lobby size if players ask.
- Hold a 20–40% intro discount for the first week.
- Reserve capacity for patching in launch week.

**Red flags:** Only unspecified "technical issues" were reported. The real risk is retention. Chaos co-op games spike hard and decay quickly, so the update roadmap is vague.

## Cross-cutting lessons for a solo developer

1. **Price $5–8 plus co-op beats $15–20 single-player on impulse.** Group purchase decisions are cheap decisions.
2. **Build the clip first.** If you cannot describe the funny or striking moment in one sentence, rethink the core.
3. **Demo early.** Silver Pines and the Next Fest leaders all had demos live before the event or launch.
4. **Know your niche's ceiling.** over the hill's studios saw the contrast with Golden Lap (~10k copies): a hardcore niche caps out.
5. **Keep launch-week patch capacity.** How to Fish's lobby-size fix landed within days.
6. **Collect wishlists for 6–12 months.** Every hit above that discloses a timeline did.
