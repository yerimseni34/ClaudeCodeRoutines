## Read this first: data quality

- Web search surfaced **only one verifiable game that launched inside the window** (Dressmaker, Sep 21). Steam Next Fest (Oct 19–26) is only a day in, so no breakout numbers exist yet.
- The other entries below are **recent (Mar–Aug 2026) hits that are still the best-documented small-team cases**. They are labelled "outside window". Include them as benchmarks, not as "this month's news".
- Many sources are aggregators/listicles. Each figure is tagged: **[confirmed]** = developer/publisher/Steam-sourced, **[reported]** = single secondary source, **[est.]** = third-party estimate or the source's own arithmetic. Anything not found is marked "not found" rather than guessed.

## 1. Dressmaker — IN WINDOW (launched Sep 21, 2026)

- **Genre / platform / price:** Cozy sewing/crafting sim · Steam (Windows, Mac), no controller support at launch · $14.99 (10% launch discount to Sep 29) [reported]
- **Team:** Cozy Lives, a small label inside Free Lives (Broforce studio); core creators credited: 4 named, plus music/writing contributors. Under 10 people [confirmed via press release]. Not strictly "indie-solo": has a publisher/parent studio.
- **Numbers:** 97% positive from 4,263 reviews and ~27.4k recent players ~1 week in (SteamDB, via PC Games Hardware, Sep 27) [reported]. Charted in Steam top sellers (#9 day one) [reported]. Revenue: not found. Devs say sales have covered costs [reported].
- **Why it succeeded:**
  - A *job fantasy* people can't do in real life (same pull as farming/truck sims) with deep, detailed systems.
  - Proven demand before launch: a ~3-week prototype passed 100k downloads; 100k → 200k+ wishlists announced with a fixed release date [confirmed].
  - Fair mid-tier price, tiny launch discount to nudge day-one buys.
- **Marketing:** Prototype virality → Steam page → press release w/ release date. No Next Fest or demo mentioned. Channels beyond that: not found.
- **Solo-replicable:** Ship a rough free prototype fast and let its download count be the proof of demand. Announce a hard date once wishlists are high. Pick a niche "tactile job" fantasy.
- **Red flags:** No controller/Steam Deck support at launch; online mode, mobile, and Switch are promised but unshipped. Prototype fame doesn't transfer without a polished full game.

## 2. Big Walk — outside window (launched Aug 4, 2026; ~11 weeks old)

- **Genre / platform / price:** Co-op exploration/puzzle · PS5, Switch 2, PC · $19.99 [reported]
- **Team:** House House (4 people), published by Panic [reported].
- **Numbers:** 1M+ in ~6 days [confirmed by Panic]; 3x faster than Untitled Goose Game (~3 months). Unclear whether PS Plus claims are counted. Metacritic 92, best-reviewed game of 2026 [reported].
- **Why it succeeded:** Brand goodwill from Goose Game, no-combat voice-chat co-op (very streamable), critical acclaim, and day-one PS Plus exposure that seems to have amplified rather than cannibalised sales.
- **Marketing:** Publisher-led; no verified channel breakdown found.
- **Solo-replicable:** Little directly (brand + platform deal). Transferable lesson: design for voice chat/co-op so players create their own content.
- **Red flags:** Players asking for matchmaking; sales-count ambiguity.

## 3. Sir, We Have an Orc Problem — outside window (July 2026)

- **Genre / price:** Incremental + tower defense · Steam · price not found.
- **Team:** 2 developers, ~4 months of development [confirmed, dev post-mortem via HowToMarketAGame].
- **Numbers:** $361,657 gross in 24h; ~200k units in 20 days; ~150k wishlists at launch (from 3k two weeks before page announcement) [confirmed by devs].
- **Why it succeeded:** "Amplify a proven hit" — took Outhold's concept and scaled enemies to 10,000. Short, scrappy and clear.
- **Marketing:** Went viral on **TikTok before the Steam page existed** (~10–15 min/day simple screen recordings, clips ~100k views); popular-YouTuber playtest; **Next Fest ≈ 18k wishlists**; only **$200 paid** (curator sale). Mostly strategy-in-execution, with acknowledged luck.
- **Solo-replicable:** Very. Post daily raw clips from week one, scale a proven loop 10×, spend almost nothing on ads.
- **Red flags:** Their previous 1.5-year game got 13k wishlists in 10 months — the team says the faster, smaller project was the lesson.

## 4. MECCHA CHAMELEON — outside window (June 2026)

- **Genre / price:** Hide-and-seek disguise multiplayer · Steam · $5.99 (one source says $4.79) [reported].
- **Team:** 1 developer, ~2 months [reported]. Concept prototyped in Fortnite Creative.
- **Numbers:** Sources conflict: 1M in 4–5 days [Steam news per TechWiser]; 5M in 10 days with 244,731 peak CCU [reported]; 10M–15M+ in other places. Treat all as **unconfirmed beyond "1M in a week"**. The $25M figure is one blog's arithmetic [est.].
- **Why it succeeded:** One-sentence concept, built-in clip moments, impulse price, Twitch/X clip spread, no ad spend [reported].
- **Solo-replicable:** Concept testing in a free UGC platform first; super-low price; make every round produce a clip.
- **Red flags:** Servers/ops at that scale and inconsistent figures; no reported problems found.

## 5. Sledding Game — outside window (Early Access Apr 30, 2026)

- **Genre / price:** Cozy proximity-voice multiplayer · Steam + Xbox/Game Pass day one · price not found.
- **Team:** Solo (Max / The Sledding Corporation).
- **Numbers:** Demo (Sept 2025): 80k wishlists first week, 120k+ players. 100k+ copies sold by day 5 [reported].
- **Why / marketing:** Building in public on YouTube/TikTok/Instagram (500k+ IG followers) + free demo + ragdoll/voice chat comedy. Press (VICE, RPS) followed. This is mostly strategy.
- **Solo-replicable:** Public devlog clips + a free demo as the funnel. A Game Pass day-one deal is not replicable on request.
- **Red flags:** None reported; demo-to-launch gap was ~7 months.

## 6. Tangy TD — outside window (March 2026)

- **Genre / price:** Vampire-Survivors-style tower defense · Steam · price not found.
- **Team:** Solo, streamer/YouTuber Cakez; ~4 years of public dev streams.
- **Numbers:** $245,123 gross / ~28k units first week; ~89% positive [reported].

## Cross-cutting patterns (supported by the above)

1. **Price band $3–$15 dominates small-team breakouts** (Meccha $5.99, How to Fish $7.99, Dressmaker $14.99). Big Walk at $19.99 had brand + publisher support.
2. **Short-form video before the store page** (Orc Problem, Sledding Game) beat paid ads. Total paid spend in the best case: $200.
3. **Streamability is a design requirement**: voice chat, hide-and-seek, ragdolls produce clips with no extra effort.
4. **Free proof-of-demand assets** (prototype downloads, free demo) preceded the biggest launches (Dressmaker, Sledding Game).
5. **Next Fest helps but isn't the engine**: ~18k wishlists for Orc Problem. Feb 2026 fest data: median demo +806 wishlists, 95th percentile +13,461, top +57,074, with pre-fest wishlists the best predictor (ziva.sh analysis) [reported].
