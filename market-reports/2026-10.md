# Indie Market Report — October 2026

*Generated 2026-10-06 by an automated routine. Window: roughly 2026-09-06 to 2026-10-06, plus games released earlier whose traction carried into the window.*

## Read this first: data quality

- Sources were mostly news aggregators and SEO-style sites. Some copy each other, and some conflict.
- **Sales and revenue numbers are approximate.** Where sources disagree, I give the range and flag it.
- **Marketing detail is thin.** For most games, no source documented Next Fest use, wishlist counts, or paid marketing. Where I write "inferred", it is my reading of the game design and not a reported fact.
- Team sizes were confirmed only where noted.
- I could not fetch full text for several articles (truncated or 403). Treat per-game "why it worked" as informed analysis, not postmortem fact.
- Excluded on purpose:
  - Peak: its sales data in the search results was from 2025.
  - Control Resonant: a Remedy title, which is AA/AAA.
  - Graveyard Keeper 2: no team or price data found, and the studio is likely over 10 people.
  - Orbitals: the "100k copies" claim could not be verified, and the game appears to be Switch 2-first.

## Macro picture

- Steam had a record September 2026, reported at about $1.7B in revenue.
- The dominant pattern is **"friendslop"**: cheap ($5–8), chaotic, physics-driven co-op games built to produce clips. These were the biggest unit-sellers.
- Quality-first cozy and job-sim games ($15) also broke into Steam's top sellers.

## Games

### 1. How to Fish — Dazed Games (2 people, Sweden)

- **Genre / platform / price:** physics co-op comedy, Steam, $7.99. The launch price was $4.95.
- **Release:** 2026-08-20.
- **Numbers:** 1M sales in 2 days. Concurrent players went from about 18k on launch day to 268k by Aug 23, with a reported peak of 340k+. Reported totals are 10.1M copies and $50M+, with about $19.3M in September alone (Alinea Analytics). It was still selling an estimated 40–50k copies a day in September. About 94% of 16k+ reviews are positive.
- **Why it succeeded:**
  - **Clip-ready physics.** Players launch fish, shoot them, and fight sharks. Every session produces a shareable moment.
  - **Price.** The launch discount cuts the barrier for a game that needs several friends to buy it (Inven Global makes this point).
  - **Depth beyond the joke.** Gear upgrades, quests, bosses, rare-fish collection and gambling mechanics keep people playing after the first laugh.
  - **Retention.** September sales held up for weeks, which points to a long tail.
- **Marketing:** no documented ad spend, Next Fest, or TikTok campaign. The viral spread came through streamers and short video, and the game was built for exactly that. (Mostly inferred.)
- **Solo-replicable:**
  - Design the core verb so a 10-second clip is funny with no context.
  - Price at $5–8 and discount at launch.
  - Add a progression layer so retention outlasts the novelty.
- **Red flags:**
  - Servers and netcode surely strain at 268k+ concurrent players. I found no reports either way.
  - A joke game can lose its audience when the novelty fades.
  - The studio publicly committed to continued content and bug fixes. That is a heavy post-launch burden for two people.

### 2. Meccha Chameleon — 2-person team (Japan)

- **Genre / platform / price:** hide-and-seek multiplayer where you paint your character to blend in, Steam. The price is reported as $5.99, or "sub-$5" in one source.
- **Release:** June 10, 2026 (sources say June 9 or 10). It is outside the 30-day window, but it remains the year's defining indie hit and a comparison point for the rest of this report.
- **Numbers:** 10M copies in 16 days, 15M in under a month, and 20M+ at about 63 days. Revenue estimates conflict: roughly $25M at 5M copies, and about $90M in one source. Treat revenue as unverified.
- **Credited team:** lemorion_1224 (maps and models) and Haganeiro (systems). Reported development time was about two months.
- **Why it succeeded:**
  - **Spontaneous hilarity.** Matches produce funny moments by design.
  - **Impulse price.**
  - **Social loop.** Streamers and X clips fed new buyers, who made more clips.
  - **Low spec.** Players can pick it up and play within minutes. Creator content, such as recreating the Mona Lisa as a painted hide-and-seek scene, kept the loop going.
- **Marketing:** reportedly no spend. There was no documented Next Fest or paid campaign.
- **Solo-replicable:** the *scope*. A 2-month build with a single-sentence pitch is feasible. The *outcome* is not repeatable. It is a lottery-ticket strategy.
- **Red flags:** none found in the sources. The usual risks of a viral multiplayer hit apply: moderation, cheating, and keeping content fresh. These are not documented here.

### 3. Big Walk — House House (4 people, Melbourne; published by Panic)

- **Genre / platform:** co-op "talking and walking" adventure. PC, Switch 2, PS5.
- **Release:** 2026-08-04.
- **Numbers:** 1M+ copies in 6 days. It launched with a 25% discount on Steam and was a PlayStation Plus monthly game.
- **Price:** I could not confirm the base price. It is probably within the user's $20 limit but unverified.
- **Why it succeeded:**
  - **Proven studio.** House House made Untitled Goose Game.
  - **Co-op social hook,** the same pattern as the friendslop hits above.
  - **Strong reviews.** It is rated among the year's best.
  - **Platform and publisher reach.** Panic published it, and the PS Plus placement and multi-platform launch widened the audience.
- **Solo-replicable: little.** It leans on prior reputation, a publisher, and a console deal. The transferable lesson is to build a distinct, easily explained co-op premise.
- **Red flags:** not found. Note that it is not a no-name solo-dev story.

### 4. BOMBANANA! — Lefto Studio (team size not disclosed; published by TARK)

- **Genre / platform / price:** 3-player co-op bomb defusal with three monkeys (blind, deaf, mute). Steam, $7.99. Launch price was $4.95 through Sept 9.
- **Release:** 2026-09-02.
- **Numbers:** 1M+ Steam units (GameDiscover). About 96% positive from 1,300+ reviews early on.
- **Why it succeeded:**
  - **Asymmetric information puzzle.** It is a clear "Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes" variant. Each player has a handicap, so shouting, gestures and thrown bananas produce funny content.
  - **Price and launch discount.**
  - **Playable with strangers.** Reviewers note it works well with randoms, which helps matchmaking-free adoption.
  - **Enough content.** The campaign has 30 levels (4–5 hours) plus an endless mode.
- **Marketing:** it has a publisher. No details on Next Fest, demo, or streamer seeding were found.
- **Solo-replicable:** a known mechanic with a twist, a launch discount, and an endless mode to extend playtime.
- **Red flags:** the 4–5 hour campaign is short, so long-term retention is unproven.

### 5. Dressmaker — Cozy Lives (under Free Lives)

- **Genre / platform / price:** cozy dressmaking job sim. Windows, macOS, Steam Deck. $14.99, with 10% off at launch.
- **Release:** 2026-09-21.
- **Numbers:** 300k+ Steam units (GameDiscover). It reached about 9th place in global top sellers within days. About 96% positive ("Overwhelmingly Positive").
- **Team:** the prototype was built in three weeks by two developers (Ruan Rothmann and Sara Laubscher). Total team size was not stated. I did not verify whether it is under 10 people, so it may not fit your criteria.
- **Why it succeeded:**
  - **Pre-launch demand.** 200k+ wishlists were announced with the release date.
  - **Tactile, satisfying craft loop.** Choosing fabric, cutting and sewing are all under the player's control.
  - **Subject-matter expertise.** A fashion-history expert co-created the game.
  - **Niche appeal.** Cozy sims have a loyal audience.
- **Marketing:** wishlist-driven. The wishlist figure came from a press release. No Next Fest or social details were found.
- **Solo-replicable:** build a tactile prototype fast, then validate with wishlists before a big build. Partnering with a domain expert is replicable. The wishlist scale is hard without publisher help.
- **Red flags:** none found.

## Cross-cutting takeaways for a solo dev

1. **The pattern in the biggest hits was a $5–8 price, a one-sentence premise, and gameplay that makes clips with no context.** Multiplayer here seems to have worked as a content engine and not only as a feature.
2. **Launch discounts on co-op games were common** (How to Fish, Bombanana, Big Walk). In co-op, one buyer needs to convince friends to buy too.
3. **Small scope can win.** Meccha Chameleon was reportedly built in two months by two people.
4. **Virality looks like luck, but the design is controllable.** Clip-friendliness, low price, and low hardware requirements are within a solo dev's control. Streamer pickup is not.
5. **Plan for success.** Viral co-op games need server capacity, bug fixes and content updates quickly. Two-person teams are publicly promising that work.
6. **Quality-first games still work at $15** when wishlists are built beforehand (Dressmaker).
