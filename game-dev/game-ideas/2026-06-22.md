# Game Concepts — 2026-06-22

For: solo Unity dev (1–2 yrs), current project = wave survival shooter.
All concepts bias toward reusing wave-survival foundations (spawners, pooling, damage numbers, juice) so existing tech transfers.

## Research signals fed into this batch

- Steam officially codified "Bullet Heaven" as a genre tag (May 2026 update). The Vampire Survivors-like is now a known shelf, fragmenting into open-world, spellslinger, and licensed variants. Saturation is real but the audience is also bigger than ever.
- Liminal-space games are flooding Steam (~600 results) — A24's Backrooms film raised the ceiling but the well is crowded; only fresh angles cut through (e.g. Farsight, set inside an optometrist's autorefractor).
- Capybara of Indifference meme is dominant on TikTok/Twitter; Capybara Simulator already shipped the "chill" angle — the indifference framing is still open.
- KitKat Heist (400k bars stolen in transit across Europe) is one of 2026's defining absurdist news stories; the heist meme is hot, no game yet.
- 2026 Olympics produced two breakout meme formats: "Villain Turned Hero" and "Covered A Lot of Ground" — redemption-arc framing has cultural currency.
- Underserved sub-genres explicitly flagged by analysts: coin-pusher roguelikes (post-dice/deckbuilder), low-stress horror, automation strategy, short repeatable sessions.
- High-signal r/gameideas posts: a Souls-like where you unlock *concepts* and combine them into weapons; "wholesome robots that turn into war machines when seen"; create-the-problem-sell-the-solution management sim.
- Schrödinger's Call (visual novel — last confidant for souls between life and death) is critically loved; the "phone calls from the dead" framing is unclaimed in action genres.

## 1. Indifference Survivor *(trend)*

- **Pitch:** A wave-survival shooter where you play a capybara who simply cannot be bothered to fight, so your accumulating retinue of stressed-out woodland animals does it for you.
- **Core mechanic:** You walk slowly toward hot springs to top up Chill. Each animal you pass while Chill is high joins your entourage and auto-shoots in their own panicked style. Lose Chill and the herd disbands mid-fight.
- **Why now:** Capybara of Indifference is the meme of the year and Steam just blessed "Bullet Heaven." The cozy/auto-shoot fusion is unclaimed — Capybara Simulator covers chill, not combat.
- **Solo feasibility:** **9/10.** Pure extension of an existing wave-shooter loop: swap player auto-attacks for retinue auto-attacks, add a Chill stat. Art is forgiving — capybara silhouettes are iconic at 32×32.
- **Scope:** 3-month MVP is realistic; 6 months for a full Steam EA build with 5 biomes.
- **Closest existing:** Brotato meets Capybara Simulator; spiritually adjacent to Death Must Die.
- **Fatal flaw:** Meme half-life — by ship date the capybara wave may have receded. Hedge by making the indifference *mechanic* fun on its own, not the joke.

## 2. KitKat Heist: Sweetline *(trend)*

- **Pitch:** Wave-defense roguelite about hijacking European candy logistics — defend the truck, shoot the cops, eat the loot to heal.
- **Core mechanic:** Each run = one stretch of highway. Auto-shooting turrets bolted onto a moving truck, with branching exits (Calais / Antwerp / Strasbourg). Eating candy bars heals but increases pursuit heat; hoarding them = score.
- **Why now:** The real-life 400k-bar heist is the absurdist news story of 2026. Heist is having a moment (Snap & Grab) but nobody's claimed the bullet-heaven version.
- **Solo feasibility:** **7/10.** Moving-vehicle shooter changes the camera contract — needs a stable scrolling pipeline. Branding has to be fictional ("KatKit," "Twixt") to dodge IP.
- **Scope:** 6 months minimum. The truck-as-arena is doable in 3 but the heist fantasy needs map variety and a getaway meta-loop.
- **Closest existing:** Brotato + Death Road to Canada + Payday minigames.
- **Fatal flaw:** Topical-news games go stale fast. If the meme dies before launch, the hook collapses to "generic heist auto-shooter."

## 3. Backrooms Bullet Heaven *(trend)*

- **Pitch:** Vampire Survivors in the Backrooms — yellow halogen corridors, hordes of partygoers, dread that scales with your DPS.
- **Core mechanic:** Standard auto-shooter run, but each level-up forces a doorway choice that shifts you to a new Backrooms level with new enemy types. Higher levels = more powerful weapons but the music distorts and Smilers spawn.
- **Why now:** Steam named the genre AND is drowning in liminal games — the *crossover* is empty. A24's Backrooms film keeps the aesthetic at peak mindshare through 2027.
- **Solo feasibility:** **9/10.** Tilesets are intentionally repetitive (the whole point), enemies are silhouettes, you already know how to build the loop.
- **Scope:** 3 months for a strong vertical slice; 6 for a full release.
- **Closest existing:** Inscryption mood + Brotato loop + Pools (the liminal walking sim).
- **Fatal flaw:** Backrooms saturation. Need a real mechanical reason the liminal aesthetic matters or it's "Brotato with yellow walls."

## 4. Pusher *(gap)*

- **Pitch:** A coin-pusher roguelite where every coin you knock off the edge becomes a bullet in the wave-defense round that follows.
- **Core mechanic:** Two phases that feed each other. Pusher phase = drop coins, watch physics, build chains. Defense phase = your earned coins are shot at incoming waves, with coin types (gold = AOE, copper = pierce, gem = chain lightning).
- **Why now:** Analysts explicitly flagged coin-pusher roguelites as the next "dice/deckbuilder"-style gap. Nobody is shipping the genuinely great one yet — early entries are mobile-feeling.
- **Solo feasibility:** **6/10.** Two distinct game loops doubles the design surface; the physics phase needs careful tuning to feel weighty. Doable, not trivial.
- **Scope:** 6 months. The two-phase loop needs real iteration; rushing it kills the game feel that justifies the genre.
- **Closest existing:** Balatro's structural rigor + arcade pushers + Luck be a Landlord.
- **Fatal flaw:** Coin-pusher physics on a budget can feel cheap. If the satisfaction of the push isn't perfect, the shooting round can't save it.

## 5. Concept Forge *(gap)*

- **Pitch:** A wave shooter where you don't unlock weapons — you unlock abstract *concepts* (Fire, Loop, Mirror, Hunger) and slot them into a forge to invent your own.
- **Core mechanic:** Three concept slots per weapon. Fire+Loop = a flame ring. Mirror+Hunger = a beam that grows stronger off enemy bullets. Synergies are emergent and the game ships with no canonical "best build."
- **Why now:** Top-of-r/gameideas post in 2026, no shipped game executing it. Players are tired of preset loadouts; deckbuilders proved the appetite for combinatorial depth.
- **Solo feasibility:** **5/10.** The forge itself is small code; the *balancing* of N-choose-3 concept combinations is the entire game and a content-design grind for a solo dev.
- **Scope:** 12 months honestly. A 6-month build will ship with too few concepts and feel hollow.
- **Closest existing:** Noita (spell crafting) + Magicka + Death Must Die.
- **Fatal flaw:** Combinatorial design is a tar pit. Without a forcing function (concept rarity, slot constraints) the metagame collapses to one dominant combo.

## 6. Nightshift Greenhouse *(gap)*

- **Pitch:** By day, a cozy automation-driven greenhouse builder. By night, the plants you grew defend the greenhouse from polite, apologetic intruders.
- **Core mechanic:** Day phase = pipes, sprinklers, plant placement (Factorio-lite). Night phase = a wave-survival round where your layout *is* your loadout; vine paths choke chokepoints, sunflowers auto-target. No micro during the wave — you watch your factory fight.
- **Why now:** Three underserved niches stacked in one game: low-stress horror, automation strategy, short repeatable sessions. Each alone has a hungry audience; the intersection is empty.
- **Solo feasibility:** **6/10.** Two systems must dovetail. The automation phase is the hardest part; grid-based saves a lot of suffering vs. free placement.
- **Scope:** 6 months for a tight one-greenhouse build; 12 for biome variety and a campaign.
- **Closest existing:** Plants vs. Zombies × Mindustry × Strange Horticulture.
- **Fatal flaw:** Cozy + tower defense has been tried; the differentiator has to be the horror tone. If the intruders aren't actually unsettling, it's just PvZ.

## 7. Last Confidant *(wildcard)*

- **Pitch:** A wave shooter where your phone keeps ringing. Each caller is a dying soul who needs you to fight through their unfinished business — and gives you the loadout shaped by the life they led.
- **Core mechanic:** Pick up the phone between waves. The voice on the line is procedurally seeded: a firefighter dies and you wave-clear with hoses and axes; a debt collector dies and you wave-clear with paperwork that staples enemies. Loadout = whoever called this run.
- **Why now:** Schrödinger's Call (the breakout VN of 2026) proved the "phone calls from the dead" framing has real emotional pull, and nobody has tried it in an action genre. The narrative-shooter hybrid is wide open post-Hades.
- **Solo feasibility:** **5/10.** Loadout-per-caller means lots of weapons; the writing burden is the actual scope risk, not the code.
- **Scope:** 12 months. The voice acting + writing budget is what eats time, not the wave loop.
- **Closest existing:** Hades meets Schrödinger's Call meets Death Must Die.
- **Fatal flaw:** If the calls don't land emotionally, it's a gimmick wrapper around a normal shooter. Needs a real writer collaborator or scope cut.

## 8. Anti-Run *(wildcard)*

- **Pitch:** A wave shooter where the protagonist starts as the villain — a tyrant overlord — and every run is the story of how the rebellion turned them around. By the final wave you're shooting *with* the people you were shooting at the start.
- **Core mechanic:** You start each run overpowered, mowing down peasants. Around wave 5 a "Turn" event triggers (player choice, scripted): your old weapons start jamming, you lose them piece by piece, and former enemies become allies with rebel weaponry. The build you end with is unrecognizable from the one you started with.
- **Why now:** "Villain Turned Hero" was THE 2026 Olympics meme format. The redemption arc is in the cultural water, and roguelites haven't done a mechanical inversion mid-run.
- **Solo feasibility:** **6/10.** The Turn mechanic is gameplay-novel — half the systems have to be designed as inverses of each other. Beautiful, but doubles the content count.
- **Scope:** 6 months for the core hook; 12 for a campaign that respects the framing.
- **Closest existing:** Tyranny's setup + Hades' run framing + Brotato's loop.
- **Fatal flaw:** If the Turn feels scripted rather than earned, the whole emotional arc fails — and the system that earns it is genuinely hard to design.

## Recommendations

If forced to pick one to prototype this week:

- **Highest hit-rate, lowest risk:** *Indifference Survivor* (#1). Reuses your current tech, rides a live meme with a real gameplay angle, ships in 3 months.
- **Best long-term IP:** *Nightshift Greenhouse* (#6). Three underserved niches stacked is a moat; the audience is hungry.
- **Most ambitious but most defensible:** *Pusher* (#4) or *Concept Forge* (#5) — coin-pusher is a market gap with a closing window; concepts are a 12-month love-letter project.

Avoid: any concept here that depends on a meme surviving 12 months. Either ship in 3 (Indifference, Backrooms, Heist) or pick a concept whose hook doesn't decay (Greenhouse, Pusher, Forge).
