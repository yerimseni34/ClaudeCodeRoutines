# Competitor Watch — Week 30, 2026 (Jul 17 – Jul 24)

Genre scope: wave survival shooters, roguelite action, top-down shooters, isometric action. Sources: Steam store/community pages, itch.io, and indie game press (Gematsu, GamingOnLinux, Chalgyr's Game Room, Gamers Heroes, COGconnected, TheSixthAxis, Saving Content).

## 1. Pass the Fear
- **Developer / team size:** PlayMud Studio (small indie team), published by ParasGames
- **Status:** Released (full 1.0 launch, exited Early Access)
- **Steam signal:** Very Positive — 84% of 341 reviews
- **This week:** Launched July 23, exiting Early Access with a "time loop conspiracy" narrative payoff — 5 full chapters, new bosses, and hidden stages, more than doubling the ~2.5 chapters the demo offered.
- **Momentum:** Rising. Clean launch-week review ratio and a co-op bullet-hell hook (170+ relics, 30 weapons, 22 tarot cards, 4 characters) giving players reasons to keep building runs together.
- **Steal this:** The Early Access → full release messaging leaned entirely on "here's what's new since the demo" (chapter count, boss count) rather than re-explaining the pitch — a good template for narrating your own content delta at launch.

## 2. Company of Heroes 3: Final Stand
- **Developer / team size:** Relic Entertainment (AA/AAA studio) — flagged because a major RTS studio is now competing directly in wave-defense roguelite territory
- **Status:** Upcoming — launches July 29 ($29.99, standalone, no base game required)
- **Steam signal:** Pre-release; wishlist campaign active, no review data yet
- **This week:** Steam Community "Coming July 29" countdown post went out, keeping the standalone wave-defense spin-off in front of RTS and roguelite audiences alike ahead of launch.
- **Momentum:** Rising (pre-launch hype). A recognizable IP applying roguelite progression to 12-round wave survival is a signal the wave-defense subgenre is now attractive enough for established studios to enter.
- **Steal this:** Positioning wave-defense as a lightweight "standalone spin-off" of a bigger franchise — no prior ownership required — is a low-friction way to pull a built-in audience into a new mode.

## 3. Appulse
- **Developer / team size:** Le Rado (solo/small team), published by Raw Fury
- **Status:** Demo (pre-release)
- **Steam signal:** No public review count yet — demo is fresh
- **This week:** Shipped an updated Steam demo with gameplay reworks, a revamped UI, a new level, and new perks/upgrades, plus a "Secret Sauce" leaderboard challenge with prizes for the top 3 that ran through July 23.
- **Momentum:** Rising. Publisher-backed iteration cycle (demo → community feedback → reworked demo) plus a competitive leaderboard event is actively manufacturing return visits during the demo phase.
- **Steal this:** The time-boxed leaderboard challenge tied to a specific demo build is a cheap way to generate urgency and repeat play before a Steam page has any reviews to lean on.

## 4. KAZ
- **Developer / team size:** Kalinarm — solo developer, published by HAKURO
- **Status:** Released (July 13 — just outside the 7-day window, but still the hottest momentum story touching this week)
- **Steam signal:** Very Positive — 96% of ~94 reviews; Metacritic peak of 93
- **This week:** Riding launch-week buzz into its final discount week — 15% intro price cut ($6.99 → $5.94) expires July 27, creating a last-chance-to-buy moment inside this reporting window.
- **Momentum:** Rising, and the strongest reviews-to-price ratio of anything found this week. A $6.99 minimalist score-attack roguelike hitting 96% positive is a reminder that scope discipline can outperform bigger, more "featured" releases.
- **Steal this:** Music that intensifies with your score, plus 25+ purely cosmetic/mechanical "themes" made in collaboration with other indie games (crossover cameos), turned a very small core loop into something people keep replaying and talking about.

## 5. Guildrun
- **Developer / team size:** Leyline — indie studio led by former Hearthstone and The Bazaar designers
- **Status:** Announced (~July 16) with a public demo already live
- **Steam signal:** No aggregate review score yet; notably the closed alpha grew to 3,000+ testers before the Steam page even existed
- **This week:** Announcement coverage (Gematsu, Niche Gamer, Saving Content) broke this week, introducing a PvE "roguelike autobattler" pitch — build a hero team, push into rifts, and grind wave-based encounters between meta-progression runs.
- **Momentum:** Rising. Pedigree (Hearthstone/Bazaar design credits) is doing a lot of the current press pickup, and the pre-announcement community-testing strategy means it launches into review season with an built-in vocal fanbase.
- **Steal this:** Opening a closed alpha years before a Steam page exists — and growing it organically to thousands of testers — front-loads word-of-mouth so the "announcement" week already has an audience instead of starting from zero.

## 6. Gunstoppable
- **Developer / team size:** CAGE Studios — small indie team (LA-based, founded 2017 by Salaar Kohari and Gabriel Langlois)
- **Status:** Demo live; full release set for August 5 (PC + Xbox Series X|S)
- **Steam signal:** Demo: Very Positive recent trend — 91% of last-30-days reviews, 82% of 35 all-time reviews
- **This week:** Continued demo momentum heading into its final pre-launch stretch, pitched as a "momentum-fueled roguelite FPS" — dual-wielding, time-bending, movement-tech (grapple/slide/wall-run/rail-grind) shooter against hordes and boss-tier "billionaire" enemies.
- **Momentum:** Rising, modest scale. Small review count but consistently positive trend as launch approaches; the movement-first FPS angle differentiates it from the top-down wave-shooter crowd.
- **Steal this:** Building the whole marketing hook around a single mechanical thesis ("speed is damage") gives every trailer beat and store-page bullet a consistent throughline instead of listing disconnected features.

## 7. Shroomwood
- **Developer / team size:** Sporelite Games, published by Zemore Indie Games
- **Status:** Demo / active playtest ahead of a planned late-2026 Early Access launch
- **Steam signal:** No aggregate score yet — demo/playtest phase
- **This week:** Continued coverage of its top-down bullet-hell demo (blending Isaac/Gungeon-style shooting with a new melee layer), keeping the "ahead of launch" playtest pipeline visible in indie press.

## Weekly Insight

**Trend emerging this week:** Wave-defense/wave-survival is visibly graduating from a pure-indie niche into a genre big studios want a piece of — Relic Entertainment (Company of Heroes) shipping a standalone roguelite wave-defense spin-off is the clearest signal. At the same time, the actual indie breakouts this week (KAZ, Pass the Fear) are winning on tight scope and a single clear mechanical hook, not scale.

**Gap that became visible this week:** Nearly every notable release this week is either a *space/sci-fi* roguelite shooter (XENOFLEKT, HYPERWIRED) or a *dungeon/forest* top-down roguelite (Shroomwood, Pass the Fear). Very few are leaning into grounded, contemporary, or "mundane-but-weird" settings the way Vampire Survivors-likes did early on — there's room for a wave-survival shooter with a distinct, non-fantasy/non-sci-fi visual identity to stand out in a crowded feed of purple nebulas and dungeon torches.

**Actionable insight for a solo dev:** Both of this week's clearest wins (KAZ at 96% positive, Guildrun's pre-launch alpha) succeeded by narrowing scope and building a testing community *before* asking for a wishlist — not by out-featuring competitors. If you're solo, a $5–7 price point with one sharply-defined mechanical hook (KAZ: score-reactive music + grid combat; Appulse: pinball-as-combat) is currently outperforming feature-stacked pitches in review sentiment, even against publisher-backed competition.
