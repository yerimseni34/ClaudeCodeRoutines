# Competitor Watch — Week 2026-W26 (June 19–26, 2026)

Genre scope: wave survival shooters, roguelite action, top-down shooters, isometric action. Coming off the tail of **Steam Bullet Fest** (June 8–15) and **Steam Next Fest** (June 15–22), so this week is mostly the aftermath: launch-week review data coming in, demos still climbing charts, and one big sequel announcement.

---

## Games Tracked

### 1. Arms of God — Dark Jay Studio
- **Team size:** Solo dev (Dominik Sójka), husband-and-wife duo, Poland.
- **Status:** Early Access (launched June 8, 2026).
- **Reviews:** 1,256 reviews, **89% positive** ("Very Positive"). Ranked Top 100 in Feb 2026 Next Fest with 88% on the demo.
- **This week:** Riding launch-week momentum from Bullet Fest exposure; multiple outlet reviews (8.5–9/10) published, praising the "five-weapon merging" build system. Devs confirmed at least 2 major content updates before 1.0 (Q4 2026/Q1 2027).
- **Momentum:** **Rising.** Strong critic scores + Doom-inspired aesthetic + runs well on Steam Deck = good word of mouth. Main knock: short core loop (3–4 hrs) and repetitive level dressing.
- **Steal this:** Weapon *merging* (5 slots, weapons fuse into upgraded versions) gives auto-battler-style build depth without needing dozens of unique items — a cheap way to make build variety feel deep with a small weapon roster.

### 2. Killer Bean — Killer Bean Studios LLC
- **Team size:** Small/indie (unspecified, branded as a long-running web animation IP turned game).
- **Status:** Early Access (launched June 8, 2026), $14.99 with launch discount.
- **Reviews:** 1,444 reviews, **74% positive** ("Mostly Positive").
- **This week:** Split critical reception — Kotaku called it "a very, very bad game," while Game8 praised its "absurd John Woo violence." Community reviews flag bugs/audio issues.
- **Momentum:** **Mixed/at risk.** Strong brand recognition (existing animation fanbase) drove initial sales, but quality complaints could cap it as a cult title rather than a breakout unless EA patches land fast.
- **Steal this:** Pre-existing IP/fanbase converted directly into day-one wishlist volume — a reminder that audience-building *before* the Steam page exists (YouTube/animation in this case) is a wishlist multiplier most solo devs skip.

### 3. Granny's Rampage — SB Choost
- **Team size:** Solo/indie (ported from itch.io/Android).
- **Status:** Released June 22, 2026. $2.99.
- **Reviews:** None yet (too new).
- **This week:** Full Steam launch after a soft-launch life on itch.io/mobile. Distinct premise (gun-toting grandmother vs. demonic suburbia) cutting through the sea of generic Vampire Survivors clones. Notably, store page discloses AI-generated art assets with human-driven design/gameplay.
- **Momentum:** **Too early to call**, but the premise commitment (not just a skin swap) is a positive signal vs. typical low-effort clones at this price point.
- **Steal this:** Itch.io/mobile-first soft launch before Steam — de-risk the concept and gather feedback cheaply before paying Steam's discovery/marketing tax.

### 4. No Such Place — ChillyRoom
- **Team size:** Mid-size studio (also publishes Soul Knight).
- **Status:** Upcoming, planned for "2026," multiplayer demo live for Next Fest.
- **Reviews:** No user reviews yet (demo-only).
- **This week:** Demo climbed to **#12 on the Next Fest Popular Demos chart** (peaked ~20th, since climbed), with concurrent demo players peaking over 3,000.
- **Momentum:** **Rising.** Top-down PvE extraction shooter pitched as "Tarkov meets Project Zomboid," with carry-over progression from demo to full release — a strong retention hook for wishlist conversion.
- **Steal this:** Letting demo progress carry into the full release turns a one-time Next Fest tourist into a returning day-one buyer — cheap retention lever for any demo-based marketing push.

### 5. Deadzone: Rogue 2 — Prophecy Games
- **Team size:** Established studio (sequel to a 2025 breakout).
- **Status:** Announced this month; "Coming Soon," demo not yet live, full release "much later."
- **Reviews:** N/A (not released).
- **This week:** Sequel announcement drew minor sequel-vs-DLC backlash (PCGamesN: devs had to publicly defend why this is a full sequel and not DLC), but also got immediate trade coverage (GameSpot, Game Informer, Polygon quotes already on the store page).
- **Momentum:** **Rising but contested.** Built-in audience from original roguelite FPS hit, but community friction over "sequel fatigue" / pricing-via-new-SKU is worth watching — could turn into wishlist hesitation if mishandled.
- **Steal this:** A clear public response to "why a sequel and not DLC" matters — community trust around monetization framing is now part of the launch narrative, not an afterthought.

### 6. 33 Immortals — Thunder Lotus Games
- **Team size:** Established studio (Sundered, Spiritfarer).
- **Status:** Exited Early Access — v1.0 launched June 10, 2026 (PC/Xbox/Epic).
- **Reviews:** Strong existing EA review base; full launch reviews still aggregating.
- **This week:** Marked the end of a long Early Access run; "33-player co-op raider" format (isometric, Diablo-adjacent) is a differentiator vs. typical 1–4 player co-op roguelites.
- **Momentum:** **Stable/rising at launch.** Long EA period built a base before 1.0 — classic "graduate from EA with proof, not promises" playbook.
- **Steal this:** Scale of co-op as the hook (33 players in one isometric raid, not 4) — a structural differentiator that's hard to copy and gives press an easy headline.

### 7. Megabonk — (context, not new this week)
- **Status:** Established hit (2.6M copies sold, ~$23.2M revenue), 95% positive across 103K+ reviews.
- **This week:** Multiplayer mode confirmed for an upcoming update; community still calling it "the" survivors-like of the moment as of June 23.
- **Momentum:** **Still rising**, nearly a year post-launch — proof that "3D Vampire Survivors" was an underserved angle, not a saturated one.
- **Steal this:** A single clear differentiator (full 3D instead of 2D bullet-heaven) outperformed a crowded field of incremental Vampire Survivors clones — genre-defining twist beats genre-perfecting polish.

## Weekly Insight

**Trend emerging:** The bullet-heaven/wave-survival space is now dense enough that Steam gave it its own tag in May, and this week shows the genre splitting into two clear lanes: (1) **commodity bullet-heaven** — cheap, fast-shipped clones like Granny's Rampage chasing volume at $3–5 price points, and (2) **structurally differentiated roguelite-shooters** — Arms of God (weapon-merge depth), No Such Place (extraction + PvE hybrid), 33 Immortals (33-player co-op scale), Deadzone: Rogue 2 (FPS + roguelite progression). The middle ground — generic top-down shooter with generic upgrades — is where reception is weakest (see Killer Bean's split reviews, Last Man Sitting's "bare bones roguelite" criticism from earlier this year).

**Gap visible this week:** Nobody in this batch combines *short-session bullet-heaven pacing* with *meaningful permanent co-op scale* (33 Immortals has scale but is a longer raid-style session; Vampire Survivors-likes have pacing but are solo/small-co-op). A 10–15 minute run, bullet-heaven-paced, designed for 8–16 player drop-in co-op, is still open.

**Actionable insight for a solo dev:** Don't compete on "more weapons/more enemies" in bullet-heaven — that lane is saturated and judged harshly the moment it looks like a clone (Killer Bean, generic top-down shooters). Instead, pick one structural mechanic that's expensive for a clone to copy in a weekend (weapon-merging, demo-to-release progression carryover, an unusual co-op player count) and make it the entire pitch line. Also: soft-launching on itch.io/mobile before Steam (Granny's Rampage) and shipping a demo with carry-over progression (No Such Place) are both low-cost ways to validate and convert before spending Steam's discovery budget.
