# Competitor Watch — Week 2026-W28 (Jul 3–10, 2026)

Genre focus: wave survival shooters, roguelite action, top-down shooters, isometric action.

## Games This Week

### 1. Company of Heroes 3: Final Stand — Relic Entertainment
- **Team size / status:** AAA studio (Relic, ~100+ devs); upcoming, launches July 29
- **Signal:** New Steam page live, wishlist push underway; no review data yet (unreleased)
- **This week:** Announced July 3 — a standalone $29.99 roguelite wave-defense spin-off of Company of Heroes 3. 12-round escalating waves + Endless Mode, 4 factions, persistent battlegroup progression between runs, co-op support.
- **Momentum:** Rising hard. Wide pickup across PC Gamer, Gematsu, TechPowerUp, GamingOnLinux the same day — a big studio validating "wave defense roguelite" as a standalone genre, not just a Vampire Survivors clone space anymore.
- **Steal this:** The "choose randomized units/abilities after each wave, keep them for the run" loop layered on top of persistent cross-run tech trees — a clean, legible way to make short arcade sessions feel like they're building toward something.

### 2. Pathogenic — Slug Disco
- **Team size / status:** Small indie team; upcoming, launches July 16 (demo already live)
- **Signal:** Full Steam page + demo live; no aggregate review count reported yet
- **This week:** Launch date locked in and pushed out across trade press. 2D top-down twin-stick roguelite where you play as a parasite conquering a human host — build via 100+ organelle/mutation combos (bullets, minion swarms, lightning, melee).
- **Momentum:** Rising — riding the tail of a strong Next Fest demo showing into a firm release date, which is usually when wishlist conversion spikes.
- **Steal this:** The "you are the disease" framing turns a generic twin-stick bullet-hell into a distinct power fantasy — reskinning familiar mechanics (waves, mutations-as-upgrades) around a single strong metaphor.

### 3. Chivalware (Demo) — Regal Pigeon (solo dev)
- **Team size / status:** Solo developer; demo live since June 1, full release autumn 2026
- **Signal:** 21 reviews, 95% positive
- **This week:** Still picking up coverage (TechTimes ran a feature July 2) as a real-time roguelite where combat and Bejeweled-style tile-matching share one grid — match tiles to charge weapons while dodging telegraphed enemy attacks.
- **Momentum:** Steady/rising for a solo project — small review count but very high positivity and continued press pickup a month after the demo dropped is a good sign of word-of-mouth durability, not a one-week spike.
- **Steal this:** Fusing a completely different genre's core loop (match-3) directly into real-time combat resource management, on the *same* screen space enemies occupy — forces one attention split instead of two separate UI panels.

### 4. Blood Dungeon — Messhof (Nidhogg creators)
- **Team size / status:** Small, well-known indie studio; demo live, launching PC/PS5/Xbox late summer 2026
- **Signal:** 95% positive demo reviews (review count not disclosed on page)
- **This week:** Continued momentum off its Next Fest demo — "bullet heaven meets platformer" positioning (parkour, wall-climbing, ceiling-hanging while kiting waves of enemies) getting compared directly to Vampire Survivors in YouTube coverage.
- **Momentum:** Rising — a recognizable studio pedigree (Nidhogg) plus a genuinely differentiated hook (verticality in an auto-shooter) is generating unprompted "X but with jump" comparisons, which is free positioning.
- **Steal this:** Adding movement-skill expression (wall-run, ceiling-hang) to a genre that's normally almost entirely about build/upgrade choices — most bullet-heavens have near-zero moment-to-moment execution skill; this one adds it back without sacrificing the wave-survival loop.

### 5. Gunstoppable (Demo) — CAGE Studios
- **Team size / status:** Small indie studio; demo live, full release August 5
- **Signal:** 82% positive demo reviews
- **This week:** Free Steam demo launched this week (Bleeding Cool, Games Press, Spanish-language outlet Kopodo all covered it same window) — momentum-fueled dual-wield roguelite FPS with grapple/slide/wall-run/rail-grind traversal and time-bend mechanics in a cyberpunk gladiator setting.
- **Momentum:** Mixed-rising — strong press pickup but 82% is noticeably softer than the other demos this week (Chivalware/Blood Dungeon at 95%), worth watching whether that holds or improves before the August 5 launch.
- **Steal this:** "Speed is damage" as the one-line pitch — movement tech isn't just traversal here, it's explicitly tied to the combat power fantasy, which gives marketing a sharper hook than "roguelite FPS."

### 6. Lootbound (Demo) — ArtDock
- **Team size / status:** Independent studio; demo live, full launch July 31 (PC + Xbox)
- **Signal:** 587 total demo reviews, 87% positive overall (95% of the last 30 days' reviews positive — trending up)
- **This week:** Nothing new this exact week, but worth flagging as the most quantitatively validated demo in this batch — it placed top-50 out of ~3,500 games at February's Next Fest (34K activations, 540 peak CCU) and review sentiment has climbed since.
- **Momentum:** Rising into launch — recent-30-day positivity (95%) outpacing lifetime positivity (87%) is a strong signal the game has been improving in response to feedback ahead of its end-of-month release.
- **Steal this:** Publishing the demo months before launch and visibly iterating on it in public — the 30-day/all-time review split is a good template for advertising "we listened" to your own wishlist audience.

### 7. KAZ — Kalinarm / HAKURO
- **Team size / status:** Small/solo team; launches July 13 (demo live)
- **Signal:** No review data yet (pre-release)

## Weekly Insight

**Trend emerging this week:** Wave-defense/survival roguelites are being validated by a AAA publisher (Relic/SEGA with Company of Heroes 3: Final Stand) at the exact same moment several solo/small-team projects (Chivalware, KAZ, Gunstoppable) are proving that a *single sharp mechanical twist* — tile-matching, 4-button-only input, movement-as-damage — is enough to stand out without a huge content budget. The genre is bifurcating: big studios chasing "more polish, more scale" wave-defense, and indies chasing "one weird hook" bullet-heaven/roguelite hybrids.

**Gap this made visible:** Almost every demo this week (Blood Dungeon, Chivalware, Gunstoppable) is scoring 82–95% positive on genuinely small review counts, which means none of them have solved discoverability yet — the mechanical hooks are landing with the players who find them, but nobody in this batch has broken out to Lootbound-scale (34K demo activations) except Lootbound itself. There's still room for a wave-survival/roguelite game with a strong single-sentence hook to win Next Fest-style visibility if it pairs the mechanic with aggressive short-form video marketing before launch, not just a Steam page.

**Actionable insight for a solo dev:** Ship the demo *early* and keep updating it publicly for months before launch, the way Lootbound and Blood Dungeon did — the review-count and recency-trend gap between "just released" (Gunstoppable, KAZ, Pathogenic — zero or thin review data) and "demo's been out a while" (Lootbound, Chivalware — climbing 30-day positivity) is the clearest differentiator in this week's data. A long public demo window, not extra content, appears to be what converts wishlist interest into review-backed momentum by launch day.
