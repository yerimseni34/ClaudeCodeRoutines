# Competitor Watch — Week 2026-W25 (June 12–19, 2026)

Scope: wave survival shooters, roguelite action games, top-down shooters, isometric action games. Most of this week's signal comes from **Steam Next Fest (June 15–22, 2026)**, which is currently flooding the genre with new demos.

## Games

### 1. Sovereign's End
- **Team:** Solo (Chris Chegwidden, ex–Cold Iron Studios Head of Development)
- **Status:** Upcoming — full release June 23, 2026; free demo live now
- **This week:** Demo dropped for Next Fest ahead of next week's 1.0 launch.
- **Momentum: Rising.** A solo dev is using the demo as a last wishlist push right before launch — textbook timing.
- **Steal this:** Abilities auto-fire; the player's only job is movement/positioning. Cuts solo-dev scope by eliminating manual attack-input design while keeping the "kiting waves" tension intact.

### 2. Deadzone: Rogue 2
- **Team:** Prophecy Games (mid-size studio, ex-Hi-Rez/AAA veterans)
- **Status:** Upcoming — sequel announced this week, free demo coming this month
- **This week:** Official sequel announcement + demo promise, while the original (8,191 reviews, 86% positive) has lost **26.5% of its 30-day player count**.
- **Momentum: Mixed.** Anticipation for the sequel is real, but it's also a sign the live-service model on the first game is cooling and they need a sequel/relaunch to reset the audience.
- **Steal this:** Announcing the sequel with an immediate free demo (not just a trailer) converts hype into measurable signal before any money is spent on marketing.

### 3. Cloudbreaker
- **Team:** 2 people (Ballast Games, Nova Scotia)
- **Status:** Demo live for Next Fest; full release targeted later in 2026
- **This week:** New Next Fest demo went live; early word-of-mouth calling it "just one more go" addictive.
- **Momentum: Rising.** A two-person team is getting unprompted press coverage by combining genres instead of competing on production value.
- **Steal this:** Mashes Vampire Survivors-style waves with FTL-style ship/inventory management. The hook is "survivors-like + deckbuilder," not better art or more content — a tiny team's only realistic differentiation lever.

### 4. Corebreaker
- **Team:** Small indie team
- **Status:** Early Access (launched May 9, 2026); demo still up
- **This week:** Steady post-launch reviews holding strong — 93% positive (136 reviews), demo at 88% positive (36 reviews).
- **Momentum: Rising/steady.** Clean demo→Early Access funnel with reviews holding up after launch, which is the hard part.
- **Steal this:** Action-platformer + roguelite progression in tight, four-biome runs — proof that a familiar single-genre mashup executed cleanly beats a novel concept executed roughly.

### 5. Lostbound
- **Team:** Tenkarider (small/solo-leaning studio) + publisher Tambù Digital
- **Status:** Upcoming — new demo + trailer dropped this week for Next Fest
- **This week:** Fresh demo and trailer specifically timed to Next Fest, getting press pickup ("Zelda-inspired").
- **Momentum: Rising buzz**, mostly on the strength of one mechanical hook getting written about everywhere.
- **Steal this:** "The world erases behind you as you're hunted" — a single, sentence-long mechanical pitch (permanent consequence + pursuit) is what's getting it coverage, not the roguelite shell around it.

### 6. Killer Bean
- **Team:** Solo (Jeff Lew, creator of the original Killer Bean shorts/film)
- **Status:** Early Access, launched June 8, 2026
- **This week:** Still riding launch-week traffic; user reviews (71% positive, 1,866 reviews) are far more forgiving than press (Kotaku: "not much fun to play").
- **Momentum: Cooling/mixed.** Built-in YouTube IP fanbase is carrying review volume, but the user/critic gap is a warning sign for retention once novelty fades.
- **Steal this — or rather, a cautionary note:** A pre-existing audience (YouTube/IP) can buy you review volume at launch, but it won't fix weak core-loop fundamentals — don't mistake launch-week numbers for product validation.

### 7. Brotato: Primal Dread (DLC)
- **Team:** Erabit Studios (small team behind the genre-defining Brotato)
- **Status:** Announced this week, paid DLC releasing later this summer 2026
- **This week:** Reveal trailer with mecha/mutant-dinosaur content for the top-down survivors-like that basically anchors the genre.
- **Momentum: Rising anticipation.** The category's biggest hit is still finding new paid-content levers two-plus years post-launch instead of relying on a sequel.
- **Steal this:** Long-tail monetization via themed content drops (new biome + enemies + items as one cohesive package) rather than piecemeal cosmetic DLC — extends a finished game's revenue life with low scope.

### 8. 33 Immortals
- **Team:** Thunder Lotus Games (established mid-size studio)
- **Status:** Released — 1.0 launched June 10, 2026 after long Early Access
- **This week:** Still in first-week post-launch attention window; 1,463 reviews at 76% positive ("Mostly Positive"), 1M+ players since EA.
- **Momentum: Mixed.** Big player numbers but review score sits below the genre's top performers — a sign the 33-player co-op isometric roguelite format still has friction (matchmaking/balance) that bigger scale alone didn't solve.
- **Steal this — or avoid this:** Scaling a roguelite to 33 concurrent players is a genuinely novel structural idea, but it shows that headcount/scale doesn't guarantee review scores in this genre — execution on session pacing matters more than player count as a selling point.

## Weekly Insight

**Trend:** Steam Next Fest (June 15–22) has turned this week into a genre-mashup showcase. Almost nothing getting coverage this week is a "pure" Vampire Survivors clone — every notable title pairs the wave/roguelite core with a second genre: ship-building (Cloudbreaker), exploration/permadeath pressure (Lostbound), platforming (Corebreaker), or large-scale co-op (33 Immortals). The plain "stand and shoot waves" formula reads as table stakes now, not a pitch.

**Gap visible this week:** No title in this batch leads with a strong narrative/character hook — everything is systems-first. There's also a clear split by team size: the smallest teams (Sovereign's End, Cloudbreaker, 2 people or solo) are winning attention precisely because they committed to one unusual genre combo instead of trying to out-produce bigger studios.

**Actionable insight for a solo dev:** Don't compete on scale against teams like Thunder Lotus or Prophecy Games. Pick one ownable, one-sentence genre mashup (the playbook every small team above used this week), and ship a free Next Fest-timed demo to validate the hook before investing further months — that's the one pattern repeating across every successful small-team entry this week.
