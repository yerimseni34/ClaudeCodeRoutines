## Weekly Insight

**Trend emerging**: The genre is fragmenting by *hybrid tag*, not by core loop. Almost nobody this week
shipped a "pure" top-down shooter or roguelite — every notable release stacked a second genre on top
(twin-stick + rhythm, bullet-heaven + adventure narrative, top-down + space-sim RPG, wave-survival + 
multiversal co-op). The core "kill waves, get loot, get stronger" loop is now table stakes; the tag combo
is the actual pitch.

**Gap that became visible this week**: Rhythm-shooter hybrids (KLAST) and narrative-layered bullet-heaven
(Dark Thoughts) both launched to near-zero press pickup despite interesting premises — the ideas are fine,
but neither had enough pre-launch wishlist-building or press outreach to be discovered on release day.
That's not a genre gap, it's an execution gap: solid hybrid concepts are being wasted by cold, no-hype
launches. The opening is for a genre-hybrid game that treats *marketing lead time* as seriously as the
mechanic — get a demo/trailer in front of press 2–3 weeks before launch instead of launching cold.

**Actionable insight for a solo dev**: Don't just pick "wave survival + X" — pick a hybrid combo that gives
press and streamers a one-sentence hook (see Gunstoppable's "speed is damage," DDoD's "fixed real-world
map instead of procgen"). Then treat your Steam Next Fest demo as the actual marketing campaign, not a
formality: DDoD and Serious Sam: Shatterverse both timed a public demo/trailer to land right before
gamescom for free press amplification — a solo dev can copy that timing discipline (align your demo drop
with a known Steam festival or industry event window) even without the budget to attend the show itself.
