## 1. Brigador Killers
**Dev:** Stellar Jockeys (small studio, founded by brothers Hugh & Jack Monahan; core team ~6–10, hired mostly from their own community)
**Status:** Early Access — launched **Aug 20, 2026**
**Traction:** 100,000+ wishlists pre-launch; demo sat at 93% positive on 400+ reviews going into launch.

**What happened this week:** Full Early Access launch on Steam + GOG on the 20th, the long-awaited follow-up to 2016's cult isometric mech-combat game *Brigador*. Adds on-foot infantry combat, vehicle warfare, base-building, and a more flexible mission structure on top of the original's tactical destruction sandbox.

**Momentum:** Rising, hard. A 9-year-old cult IP converting 100k wishlists + a 93%-positive demo into an EA launch is a textbook "patient niche audience cashes in" story — this is the playbook for reviving dormant IP rather than chasing a new one.

**Steal this:** They ran the *demo* itself as the wishlist-conversion engine for nearly a year before launch instead of a marketing blitz — the demo's review score became the pre-launch trust signal that did the selling.

## 2. DDoD
**Dev:** The Future Entertainment Company (Ukrainian-Canadian, ~15 people)
**Status:** Demo (free, live through Aug 24 as part of Ukrainian Games Festival 2026)
**Traction:** ~50,000 wishlists, ranked ~806th most-anticipated on Steam out of 4,000+ tracked projects.

**What happened this week:** Updated Steam Next Fest-style demo went live Aug 17 as part of the Ukrainian Games Festival (680 games, 50 new reveals). The demo is the product of 7 months and 11 rounds of closed playtesting with nearly 50,000 testers.

**Momentum:** Rising steadily, not viral. A top-down roguelite shooter pitched as "Diablo with guns in a STALKER world" — its hook is a **fixed, real-topography 60km² map** instead of procedural generation, a direct structural break from Gungeon/Hades-style roguelites.

**Steal this:** Betting the whole differentiation on "no procedural generation, one real place" is a cheap-to-market, hard-to-copy identity in a genre where everyone else's pitch is "more random rooms."

## 3. Survival Log
**Dev:** Midnight Workshop, published by Lilith Games
**Status:** Early Access — launched Aug 12
**Traction:** 79% positive of 1,088 reviews; launch-week peak of 10,425 concurrent players (several times a typical demo-week peak for this genre).

**What happened this week:** Score volatility — after launch, bugs and a missing save system briefly tanked the review score to as low as ~17% before the dev publicly apologized and shipped fixes, recovering the aggregate to ~79% positive within the week.

**Momentum:** Recovering, actively watch. This is a live case study in review-bomb-to-recovery: a public, fast, specific apology (naming the save-system fix) reversed a cliff-dive in days rather than weeks.

**Steal this:** The recovery playbook — acknowledge the exact bug publicly, commit to a concrete fix (not "we hear you"), and ship it fast. Worth studying their patch-note language directly.

## WEEKLY INSIGHT

**Trend emerging this week:** Two structural bets are visibly diverging from the standard "procedural roguelite" formula: (1) fixed, hand-authored worlds instead of procgen (DDoD), and (2) hybrid loops that bolt a second genre onto horde-survival (Wardens of Avalon's base-building, Survival Log's hoarding-sim layer). The wave-survival/roguelite space is crowded enough now that "just more Vampire Survivors" isn't the story anymore — differentiation is coming from structure, not just theme or art style.

**Gap made visible this week:** Survival Log's near-disaster (17% → 79% in days) shows how thin the margin is for launch-week technical execution in this genre — players in this space are primed to forgive almost anything except broken saves/progression, and just as quick to forgive it once you fix it fast and say so publicly. There's a visible gap for a wave-survival/roguelite title that ships a rock-solid save/progression system as its actual marketing hook ("your run is never lost") rather than an afterthought.

**Actionable insight for a solo dev:** Pick one structural break from the procgen-roguelite default (a fixed map, a bolted-on second loop, a guaranteed-safe save system) and make *that* the one-sentence pitch — this week's standouts (Brigador Killers, DDoD, Wardens of Avalon) are all getting covered for a single differentiator, not for overall polish. Then treat your demo as the actual sales funnel: Brigador Killers and DDoD both spent months converting demo players into wishlists before ever asking for a purchase.
