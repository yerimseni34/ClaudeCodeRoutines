Scope: wave survival shooters, roguelite action, top-down shooters, isometric action.
Data snapshot: Friday Nov 13, 2026. Review counts are Steam "all languages" figures as shown on store pages or reported by press that day; they move hourly.

- Steam wishlist counts are not public. None of these developers published a figure this week, so review counts are used instead.

## Games

### 1. Warhammer Survivors

- **Developer / team:** Auroch Digital (Boltgun studio), self-published. Mid-size studio, 100+ staff (est.), not an indie-scale team.
- **Status:** Released Nov 10. Rogueliker's calendar lists PC, consoles and mobile; Nerdly reports the Steam build is Windows only.
- **Numbers:** Mixed, 68% positive of ~1,830 reviews after three days. Daily peak 14,098 concurrent players; entered Steam's weekly most-played chart at #95.
- **This week:** Launch. The rating slid from Mostly Positive (72% of ~920 on Nov 11) to Mixed (68%) by Nov 13.
- **Momentum:** Large audience, deteriorating sentiment. The licence delivered players; the game is not holding their goodwill. Critics call it a near-copy of Vampire Survivors (Shacknews: "a reskinned Vampire Survivors") and GamingOnLinux says balance is "wildly off" across characters and weapons.
- **Study / steal:** Roster breadth as the pitch. "40+ champions" is the headline feature and it works for acquisition. The counter-lesson matters more: a known brand at $8 still lands Mixed when balance is off and there is no new mechanic.

### 2. Wielders of the Essence

- **Developer / team:** Lavapotion (Songs of Conquest), published with Coffee Stain. Small studio (est. 10–20).
- **Status:** Released Nov 5 at $5.99.
- **Numbers:** Very Positive, 93% of 509 reviews (94% of 301 on Nov 9, so the score is holding as volume grows).
- **This week:** First full week on sale. Reviews landed from Checkpoint Gaming (7/10), Gamingscope (89/100) and Rogueliker (positive).
- **Momentum:** Rising. Review count grew ~70% in four days with no drop in score.
- **Study / steal:** The store pitch states the scope outright: "Eight-minute runs. Eight hours to complete." A finite, honest size at a $6 price removes the "is there enough content?" objection before it is raised. Town-building between runs also makes meta-progression visible rather than a stat menu.

### 3. Brotato: Primal Dread (DLC)

- **Developer / team:** Blobfish. Brotato began as a solo-dev project; current team size unconfirmed.
- **Status:** Released Nov 2 at $3.99, PC only.
- **Numbers:** Mixed, 48% positive of 1,217 reviews.
- **This week:** Second week on sale and the score has not recovered. No developer balance response surfaced in my searches.
- **Momentum:** Falling. Per an aggregator summary of ~490 reviews, complaints centre on the new Evolution stat (brutal early waves, then runaway late power), unexplained and sometimes harmful mutations, pickups that force risky movement, weak new weapons, and late-wave lag. Players do like the new characters, enemies and music.
- **Study / steal:** A warning rather than a model. The content volume (10 characters, 17 weapons, 40 items, new 20-wave map) was praised; the new system that altered the base game's power curve was not. Add content along the existing curve, and test new systemic stats in a beta branch first.

### 4. Void Reaver

- **Developer / team:** Banana Blitz (debut game, self-described small team, "no AI art"). Three publishers attached.
- **Status:** Released Nov 11 at $8.99 (−20% launch discount).
- **Numbers:** 83% positive of 36 reviews (was 75% of 12 earlier in the week).
- **This week:** 1.0 launch after a February Next Fest demo.
- **Momentum:** Slow start. Sentiment is fine but volume is low for a game with three publishers and nine months of demo exposure. A demo reviewer noted early runs "offer little guidance".
- **Study / steal:** Backpack-grid inventory as the build system: 300+ items whose power comes from adjacency. The whole hook is readable in one screenshot. The weak volume suggests "bullet heaven + backpack" is already a crowded combination.

### 5. Engagement

- **Developer / team:** Trevor Parker / Trevor Games. Appears to be a solo developer (unconfirmed).
- **Status:** Released Nov 12 at $8.99 (−20% until Nov 19). Demo available.
- **Numbers:** Positive, 87% of 32 reviews within about a day.
- **This week:** Launch.
- **Momentum:** Early but healthy. It matched Void Reaver's review count in half the time with no publisher.
- **Study / steal:** Upgrades are physical. "Every upgrade is a turret bolted to a hardpoint you can see," and escorts keep their names and kill counts. Also the only launch this week with co-op (online, LAN and split-screen) and a thorough accessibility list (colourblind palettes, reduced flashes, remapping, Deck preset).

### 6. Revenant Survivors

- **Developer / team:** Mongma Studio (Seoul), debut game. Small team (size not stated).
- **Status:** Early Access since Nov 9 at $11.99 (−20% until Nov 23). Planned 3–4 months in Early Access.
- **Numbers:** Mixed, 45% of 11 reviews.
- **This week:** Early Access launch, following an August playtest and a September demo.
- **Momentum:** Stalling. Eleven reviews in four days is very low, and the score is negative-leaning. A launch-day review faults attacks interrupting movement, which reads as sluggish in this genre. Demo press had been positive but called it confusing because of the number of subsystems.
- **Study / steal:** The hook is strong: kill a boss, then take its skills or its body. The execution lesson is that large content counts (35 maps, 38 bosses, 100 items) did not compensate for movement feel, at the highest price in this list.

### 7. runz

- **Developer / team:** sardin-ventures. Likely solo or very small (unconfirmed).
- **Status:** Demo out; full game listed "Q4 2026". Rogueliker's calendar gave Nov 13, but Steam still showed it unreleased when checked.
- **Numbers:** No reviews yet. Four Steam curators have covered it.
- **This week:** Expected launch window; GamingOnLinux previewed it positively in October.
- **Momentum:** Building, modestly. Niche press interest, no visible breakout.
- **Study / steal:** The magazine is the build. You draft bullets like an auto-battler, merge three of a kind, and choose firing order across 20 waves. It is a manual-aim twin-stick shooter where build-crafting lives in ammo order, which keeps aiming skill relevant.

### 8. KILLDOZER SIMULATOR

- **Developer / team:** wildgoose. Appears solo; the developer describes working on it for nearly a decade.
- **Status:** Free demo out; launches Dec 1.
- **Numbers:** No reviews yet.
- **This week:** Demo update added collapsing buildings, flipping cars, better fire and smoke, and an accessibility tab. The Dec 1 date was announced Nov 2.
- **Momentum:** Building. Demo feedback threads are active but split; one questions replay value.
- **Study / steal:** Inverts the genre. You are the boss, the objective is a list of buildings, and the environment (gas stations, propane tanks) is your weapon against the responders. Leaderboards ship with replays and exportable video highlights, which gives players something to share.

### 9. Amazing Slot Survivor (A.S.S.)

- **Developer / team:** TWOMENGAMES (name suggests a two-person team; unconfirmed), published by LINE Games.
- **Status:** Demo out. Publisher announced Nov 13; Steam still showed "Q4 2026" when checked.
- **Numbers:** No reviews. Publisher says the demo page drew about 80,000 visits during October Next Fest.
- **This week:** Announced launch date.
- **Momentum:** Building. Publisher-backed, with a joke name that gets coverage on its own.
- **Study / steal:** A gamble as the comeback mechanic. When a run is about to end, you spin: a good result revives you, a bad one ends the run, and a jackpot makes you 18× stronger. It turns the moment of death into the most exciting moment of the run.

### Also noted

- **Delve Survivors** (iDiOSLAB, 1.0 on Nov 12, $12.99): turn-based grid roguelike with survivors swarms. Only 4 reviews a day in.
- **Nova Survivors** (PandaRojas Studios, Nov 5, $4.99): space horde shooter, 21 ships, 100-level campaign. No traction signal found.
- **Ball x Pit: Risen Ballbylon** (Nov 12): expansion for the brick-breaker/survivors hybrid, all platforms.
- **Doom: The Dark Ages "Slaughter Mode"** (Nov 16): free roguelike wave mode with a points-wagering system. AAA is now shipping wave-survival modes as free updates.

## Weekly insight

### Trend

Every notable release this week is "survivors plus one other system": base-building (Wielders), backpack grid (Void Reaver), auto-battler bullet drafting (runz), slot machine (A.S.S.), turn-based grid (Delve), capital-ship hardpoints (Engagement). Plain horde survival with a new skin is what got punished: Warhammer Survivors has the biggest audience and a Mixed rating.

Two secondary patterns:

- **Price band is $5–12 with a 10–20% launch discount, and the cheapest scored best.** Wielders at $5.99 has 93%; Revenant at $11.99 has 45%.
- **Demo first, then launch.** All nine games had a public demo or playtest before release. It is now the baseline, not an advantage.

### Gap

- **Manual-aim shooters are nearly absent.** Of roughly a dozen genre releases this week, only runz and KILLDOZER SIMULATOR ask the player to aim. I found no notable isometric action roguelite launch or announcement at all. The space is saturated with auto-attack games and thin on twin-stick skill shooters.
- **Co-op.** Only Engagement shipped with it, and it out-paced a three-publisher competitor on review velocity.
- **Environment as weapon.** KILLDOZER's destructible town is the only entry where the level itself is part of the build.

A manual-aim, top-down co-op shooter with one legible fusion hook would have had no direct competitor this week.

### One actionable insight for a solo dev

Scope small, finish it, and say so on the store page. Wielders of the Essence stated its size ("eight-minute runs, eight hours to complete"), charged $6, and has the best reception of the week. Revenant Survivors offered 35 maps and 38 bosses in Early Access at twice the price and is at 45%, with movement feel as the cited reason. Spend the saved content time on how moving and shooting feel in the first two minutes, and pick one hook that reads in a single screenshot.
